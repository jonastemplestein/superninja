Sensei's slow way to say a word used to be one smooth, stretched take (mmmaaat). Now it is the game's own pure sounds said one at a time, with a 250 ms gap between them: c · a · t. Tap Before and After for each word. The sound chips light up on each sound's onset from SLOW_TIMES, the same way a scene can light its cards. The 40 lines where Sensei explains the slow way and the fast way are further down, after the lines retaken for their accent.

Why 250 ms. It keeps every sound clearly apart but in one steady beat, about two sounds a second (a three-sound word takes 1.1–2.0 s, 1.5 s on average), and it matches the game's own tile-by-tile blend (260 ms), so the slow word and the tiles share a rhythm. At 320 ms a quick /t/ or /k/ sits alone between two long silences, the way a hiccup does, and a five-sound word runs to 2.7 s. 180 ms works too, but it gives a three-year-old less time per sound. In a blind A/B/C test on eight words the AI listener picked 250 ms most often (7 of 16) and called 320 ms too long most often (9 of 16). Your ear decides: the three gaps are side by side below the list.

Short vowels, and a voiced /g/ and /b/Updated

An AI listener heard hot as “ought”, cup as “carp” and bus as “pass”. The pure /o/ and /u/ had been stretched to 0.41 s, and in British English length is what tells /o/ (hot) from /or/ (fork), and /u/ (cup) from /ar/ (car). They are now natural short vowels cut from Sensei's own “hop” and “up”, not stretched (0.13 s and 0.11 s), and no short vowel in a slow word runs past 0.24 s. And a /g/ or /b/ on its own was heard as /k/ or /p/, so in a slow word each now starts with a short voiced closure: 45 ms of its own voicing, quietly, before the release. Before is this morning's slow word; after is what the game plays now. Your ear decides.

For your ear: British or not?New

In Southern British English “fast”, “last”, “after” and “ask” have the long ah of “father”, not the short a of “cat”, and there is no r in “word” or “first”. Two AI listeners disagreed about the first seven lines: one passed them, the other heard them as American or in between. So there is now one listener, calibrated before every run on the same sentences read by British voices (ElevenLabs Alice and George) and an American one (OpenAI coral); if it can't tell them apart, it refuses to judge. Since this morning Sensei is Gemini's Erinome voice, so every new take is Erinome. Each line was taken again, up to 20 times a wording, and a take was kept only when at least 80% of 42 votes said British on every word. Erinome's accent depends on the whole sentence: she gave “word” an American r in all 20 takes of “Every word is made of sounds, one by one.”, and a British one in 5 of 6 takes with “little sounds”. So four lines are lightly reworded; the old words are under the old takes. Listen from left to right; the last one is what the game plays.
