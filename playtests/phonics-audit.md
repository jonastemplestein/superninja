# Phonics content audit

**Audience and standard:** British children aged 4–7; Southern British pronunciations for the recorded model; Sounds~Write sound-first teaching. **Checks run:** `bun src/content/validate.ts` printed `All story text decodable ✓`; the requested `WORDS` segmentation dump was inspected word by word. The pass is a spelling-availability check, not a pronunciation check. No source files were changed.

## 1. Words and sound–spelling mappings — highest priority

1. **`<x>` is being taught as one “sound” `/ks/`, although it represents two sounds, /k/ then /s/.** This affects **box, fox, six, fix, mix, wax** (unit 7), **next, text** (unit 10), and the child-read name **Rex** (story 3). The dump gives, for example, `b/b o/o x/ks` and `n/n e/e x/ks t/t`. Treat `<x>` as one spelling that accounts for **two phonemes**, with two spoken beats and two sound positions; do not add a `/ks/` flower or tell a child it is one sound. Update segmentation, blending, sound buttons, and sound-swap logic together. Alternatively remove these words until that representation is supported. [`phonics.ts:60, 84, 182–185, 211–212`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L60); [`stories.ts:75–76`](../src/content/stories.ts#L75); [`Tree.tsx:16–24`](../src/scenes/Tree.tsx#L16). Sounds~Write's Initial Code objective lists `<x>` in unit 7, but its one-sound/two-letter concept applies to doubled consonants, not to `/ks/`. [Initial Code objectives](https://sounds-write.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/ic-objectives-1.pdf).

2. **`<qu>` is likewise compressed into the invented single phoneme `/kw/`.** Exact words: **quick, quiz, quit, quilt, squid, quack** (unit 11) and **queen** (unit 12). The dump gives `qu/kw`; the child should hear and segment /k/ /w/ (`<q>` for /k/, `<u>` for /w/) before the remaining sounds. Re-segment those seven words; replace the `kw` phoneme and single `qu` sound tile with two linked spellings/sounds. Remove them from dictation until the UI can make both sounds audible. [`phonics.ts:67, 86, 217, 224–225, 234`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L67); [`worlds.ts:100`](../src/content/worlds.ts#L100). Sounds~Write explicitly specifies `<q>` and `<u>` for /k/ and /w/ in Initial Code unit 11. [Initial Code objectives](https://sounds-write.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/ic-objectives-1.pdf).

3. **`with` has a single forced `/ð/` pronunciation.** Both /wɪð/ and /wɪθ/ occur in British English; a Southern British recording can use /ð/, but the game should accept or model the chosen recorded form consistently and should not mark a child's /θ/ as an error. Keep `with` only after voiced and unvoiced `<th>` have both been introduced; otherwise use an unambiguous word such as **this** or **thin** for the first exercise. [`phonics.ts:64–65, 221`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L64). [Oxford Learner's Dictionary: *with*](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/with).

4. **The child-read names/words `Baron` and `ninja` conceal unstressed vowels and syllables.** **Baron** is approximately /ˈbærən/; **ninja** /ˈnɪndʒə/. Neither is a transparent Initial Code single-syllable word, and neither has a sound-by-sound model here. Move **“Baron”** on story 6 read pages and **“ninja”** on story 4 page 5 to narrator-read text, or explicitly teach their syllables and schwa later. A usable page-5 replacement is “Frog and I sit on the log. Drift, log, drift!” (with `<I>` taught in context). [`phonics.ts:243`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L243); [`stories.ts:103, 141, 143, 147`](../src/content/stories.ts#L103). Sounds~Write treats polysyllabic words and schwa with explicit syllable-level instruction. [High-frequency-word guidance, pp. 8–9](https://sounds-write.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/3-high_frequency_words_in_letters_and_sounds.pdf).

5. **Vocabulary/picture checks before recording or generating more art:** **nab** (slang “catch”), **jot** (usually requires “jot down”), **prod** (a less familiar verb), **grump** (usually “a grump”), **blimp** (unfamiliar airship), and **hog** pictured as a *warty* hog are less reliable for four-year-olds who must spell from a spoken word or picture. Replace **nab → bag**, **jot → jet**, **prod → pram**, **grump → grub**; remove **blimp** pending a familiar replacement; and use an ordinary pig for **hog** or remove it. These are suitability recommendations, not pronunciation errors. [`phonics.ts:153–154, 177–179, 204–205, 211–213`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L153).

6. **Four unit-10 words are a further jump in word length:** **sprint** /s p r ɪ n t/, **prompt** /p r ɒ m p t/, **strand** /s t r æ n d/, and **script** /s k r ɪ p t/ each need **six** phonemes; the unit's other words largely rehearse five. Move these four to an explicit six-sound review after five-sound words are secure. [`phonics.ts:209–214`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L209). The Sounds~Write Initial Code objectives describe unit 10 as work on five-sound words. [Initial Code objectives](https://sounds-write.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/ic-objectives-1.pdf).

**Accent check:** The listed short-`a` words, including **hand, sand, stand, strand, lamp, camp, stamp, strap, trap, pram**, are TRAP /æ/ in Southern British English; do **not** change them to BATH /ɑː/ merely because they contain a nasal or a consonant cluster. The listed `<u>` words use Southern British STRUT /ʌ/; they are not FOOT /ʊ/ errors under the stated accent. Apart from the two-sound spellings above and the stated `with` variation, I found no other wrong mapping in the supplied `WORDS` dump. [`phonics.ts:6–7, 190–213`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L6).

## 2. Child-read stories and choices

1. **The validator's “decodable” result hides every entry in `SPECIAL_WORDS`.** It silently exempts them, so **is, a, the** occur from story 1 without any phoneme-level support; later child pages add **his**, **to**, **so**, **I**, **you**, **he**, **go**, **Baron**, and **ninja**. On tap, the `ReadWord` component plays each exempt word in full and draws no sound buttons. Keep unavoidable function words, but show their known spellings/sounds and explicitly supply only the unfamiliar correspondence in context: e.g. **is** /ɪz/ (`<s>` says /z/), **his** /hɪz/, **a** /ə/, **the** /ðə/, **to** usually /tə/ in a sentence. Do not teach the entire list as visual wholes. [`phonics.ts:242–243`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L242); [`validate.ts:5, 12–15, 37–40`](../src/content/validate.ts#L5); [`Story.tsx:138–168`](../src/scenes/Story.tsx#L138). Sounds~Write advises telling pupils the sound of the as-yet-unknown spelling, then letting them decode the word. [High-frequency-word guidance, p. 1](https://sounds-write.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/3-high_frequency_words_in_letters_and_sounds.pdf).

2. **There is no unlisted code problem in the choice words:** **pit/mat, pig/den, web/box, jam/belt, kick/tap, snail/goat** are available at their story units. **Rex** on story 3 page 2 needs the `<x>` fix above. **drift, still, click, own, sad, read, eats, yuck, sits, pops** are generated rather than in the curated word list but are genuinely segmentable by their story's final unit; **pops, eats, sits** correctly end /s/, not /z/. Add these exact generated words to an audited story vocabulary so the validator checks the *intended* pronunciation, rather than only whether a greedy spelling parse succeeds. [`stories.ts:17, 41, 64, 88, 109, 130`](../src/content/stories.ts#L17); [`validate.ts:11–27`](../src/content/validate.ts#L11).

3. **Story 4's child sentence says “get to the pond” while the narrator and art show a river.** This is a meaning/comprehension mismatch, not a code error. Change it to “Frog must get on the log” (all words supported by this point), or make the narrator and scene depict a **pond**. [`stories.ts:90, 97–105`](../src/content/stories.ts#L90).

4. **Story 2's “Peg is not in it” points back to a pig**, although the choice is meant to be a place. Change the art/choice to a **pig pen**, then teach **pen** (already unit 4) as the choice word, or change the child line to “Peg is not in the pig pen.” [`stories.ts:45–56`](../src/content/stories.ts#L45); [`phonics.ts:161–162`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L161).

## 3. Sequence, teaching, and corrections

1. **Voiced `<th>` is used but never separately taught.** The unit-11 word list correctly distinguishes **this, that, them, then, with** using `/ð/`, but the first unit-11 dojo teaches `<th>` only through its default /θ/. The tree and incorrect-tile feedback also retrieve only that default. Add a clear, short voiced /ð/ teaching step with **this/that/then**, keep /θ/ with **thin/thick**, and choose the feedback sound from the actual word, not from `GRAPHEMES["th"]`. [`phonics.ts:64–65, 86, 221`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L64); [`worlds.ts:96–102`](../src/content/worlds.ts#L96); [`Dojo.tsx:69–80, 110–130`](../src/scenes/Dojo.tsx#L69); [`Tree.tsx:16–24`](../src/scenes/Tree.tsx#L16).

2. **Dictation distractors can have the *same* sound as the answer, yet the correction says “That's [sound] ... We need [same sound].”** Examples include **`c/k/ck`, `ch/tch`, `w/wh`, `v/ve`, `ai/ay`, `ee/ea`, `igh/ie`, `oa/ow`**. This happens when the correct spelling is removed from the tile bank and another spelling of the target sound remains. Accept a spelling if it makes an appropriate real word, or say “Yes, both can spell /.../; in *this word* we write it `<...>`,” with a position/pattern cue when useful. Keep “That's /p/ ... we need /t/” for genuinely different sounds. [`learner.ts:63–77`](../src/engine/learner.ts#L63); [`Dojo.tsx:203–205`](../src/scenes/Dojo.tsx#L203); [`Battle.tsx:140–168`](../src/scenes/Battle.tsx#L140); [`Swap.tsx:107–114`](../src/scenes/Swap.tsx#L107).

3. **The correction when a child selects the wrong position in Sound Swap stops at “That's /h/ ... Listen: hat, hot.”** It does not say that /h/ stayed the same or which sound changed, despite the code comment claiming it does. Follow the words with “/h/ stays; /a/ changes to /o/. Try the middle sound,” with sound clips and the relevant tile highlight. The story question's generic “not quite” could likewise say what the picture shows and reread the relevant narrated sentence. [`Swap.tsx:65–73`](../src/scenes/Swap.tsx#L65); [`lines.ts:38–45`](../src/content/lines.ts#L38); [`Story.tsx:256–269`](../src/scenes/Story.tsx#L256).

4. **The “pure sound” recording prompts risk adding vowels or using the wrong British vowel.** `/w/` is solicited as “wwoo/woo”, `/y/` as “yyee/yee”, and `/kw/` as “kwoo/coo”; the `<a>` candidates include “aah” (/ɑː/) and `<o>` includes “oh” (/əʊ/) and IPA `ɑ` (not the target /ɒ/). A judge prompt cannot guarantee the saved clip matches it. Audit the actual audio with a Southern British phonics teacher; replace these candidates with short, accurately modelled transitions and retain the intended /æ/ and /ɒ/ clips only. Do not call a pronounced “woo/yee/coo” a pure consonant. [`phonics.ts:38–42, 58–67`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L38).

5. **The Extended Code is compressed into one local “unit 12”, and its final dojo introduces four spellings for two new vowel sounds at once.** The game sorts **`ai/ay`, `ee/ea`, `igh/ie`** but has no **`oa/ow`** sorting level. Stage /ae/, /ee/, /oe/ and /ie/ in separate, well-reviewed sound units, add an `oa/ow` sort, and label the local unit numbers so they are not confused with Sounds~Write Extended Code numbers. In the published scope and sequence, /ae/ is Extended Code unit 1, /ee/ unit 2, /oe/ unit 4, and /ie/ unit 11. [`phonics.ts:230–239`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L230); [`worlds.ts:108–119`](../src/content/worlds.ts#L108). [Sounds~Write Extended Code scope and sequence](https://sounds-write.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Extended-Code-Scope-and-Sequence.pdf).

6. **“Two letters, one sound” is overgeneralised.** It is right for `<sh>`, `<ck>`, `<ff>` and similar spellings, but not `<qu>`; `<x>` is the converse: one letter representing two sounds. Adjust both the spoken line and the sound-petal model to allow these exceptions. The “sounds squashed up close together” line for clusters is understandable, but “Say every sound” should be paired with all individual consonant sound buttons; avoid calling a cluster a single sound. [`lines.ts:51–53, 60–63`](../src/content/lines.ts#L51); [`Dojo.tsx:77–80`](../src/scenes/Dojo.tsx#L77); [`phonics.ts:182, 217`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L182).

7. **Validation needs a pronunciation-aware story list and level-by-level check.** `decode()` accepts any greedy spelling parse and returns early for every special word; it cannot flag a wrong sound, weak-form schwa, or a correspondence introduced later within the same numbered unit. Validate the actual `Word.segs` or explicit story-only segments against a reviewed pronunciation, check each story against `knownSpellings()` at its precise position, and audit the exception words separately. The present structural guard has value, but its success message overstates what was checked. [`validate.ts:11–48`](../src/content/validate.ts#L11); [`worlds.ts:143–157`](../src/content/worlds.ts#L143).

8. **A child can tap “I read it!” without reading and receive a “Well read!” and clean-read mastery for every curated word on the page.** The game cannot verify speech here; mark the page as completed but avoid recording unobserved word accuracy. Give a grown-up an optional way to confirm, or base reading mastery on a task with an observable response. [`Story.tsx:175–207`](../src/scenes/Story.tsx#L175).

9. **The first dojo presents the spelling tile as it says “This is how we write the sound.”** For a consistently sound-first lesson, play the isolated sound first, ask the child to say it and hear it in a simple word, then reveal and trace the spelling. Keep the later blending and word-building work. [`Dojo.tsx:69–105`](../src/scenes/Dojo.tsx#L69); [`lines.ts:55–60`](../src/content/lines.ts#L55).

## 4. British localisation

- **`sled`** is understandable in Britain but **sledge** is the more usual child word; because **sledge** needs untaught `<dge>`, remove **sled** from assessed word dictation or retain it with a clearly named picture and British recording. **`truck`** is also understood but **lorry** is the usual British everyday word; because *lorry* needs later code, use **duck/rock** for `<ck>` practice and leave trucks in narrator speech if desired. [`phonics.ts:203, 222`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L203).
- **`cap`** is pictured as a *baseball cap*; “cap” is fine British vocabulary, but use a generic peaked cap in the picture prompt. **`cab`** is pictured as a black taxi cab; **black cab** is British, so this is acceptable. **`crisp`**, **`mum`**, **`pram`**, **`vest`**, **`rubbish bin`**, and **`hob`** are appropriately British. [`phonics.ts:151–154, 168–171, 185–196, 202, 211`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L151).
- Ensure the generated word and sentence recordings use **/ɒ/** in **pot, box, fox, dog, fog** and **/ʌ/** in **cup, run, must**. The metadata asks for Southern British vowels, but this audit did not listen to the audio files, so this remains an audio QA item rather than a confirmed error. [`phonics.ts:38–42`](../src/content/phonics.ts#L38).
