# Pedagogy: the first two hours

The problem, in Jonas's words: the jump from "let's learn s, a, t" to "spell a three-letter word" is far too fast. There are no beginner games (telling words apart, "which of these has /a/ in it?"), and there is far too little explanation.

This doc sets out what the research and Sounds~Write actually do, and turns that into a concrete, ordered progression for Bamboo Village. It is written to be implemented.

---

## (a) Key findings

### How Sounds~Write starts Reception

1. **The first lesson is word building, not flashcards.** On day one the teacher puts three spelling cards, jumbled, above three lines and builds *sat* with the class. She says the word slowly and stretched ("sssaaat") while sweeping her finger under the lines, then asks "What's the first sound you hear in 'sat'? Listen to what you hear when my finger is under this line." Only then: "Which of these is the way we spell /s/?" If a child doesn't know, the teacher tells them: "It's this one! This is /s/. Say /s/ as you pull it onto the line." This repeats for /a/ and /t/. The class then says the sounds and reads the word, and writes it while saying each sound. There are **only three cards (no distractors)**, and the first word **starts with a continuant** (/s/, /m/), a sound you can stretch so every child can hear it. The teacher **must not segment the word for the children**, because that is their job. She stretches it and gives them a place to listen. ([Walker, *Word building: the foundation stones*, Literacy Blog 2020](https://theliteracyblog.com/2020/12/20/word-building-the-foundation-stone-of-beginning-literacy/); [Walker, *Linguistic phonics: a practical example*, 2014](https://theliteracyblog.com/2014/01/12/linguistic-phonics-a-practical-example/))
2. **Sounds~Write rejects teaching letters as isolated sound–symbol pairs.** Walker describes telling children "such and such 'makes' or 'says' this sound" as paired-associate learning that is "very difficult for young children". Sounds are learned in real, known words ([Walker 2020](https://theliteracyblog.com/2020/12/20/word-building-the-foundation-stone-of-beginning-literacy/)). The school deck says the same: letter sounds are taught "in the context of words rather than isolated sounds", with "no actions, songs or visuals to cloud the learning" (YR parents' deck, p4).
3. **The pace is two weeks per unit, and mastery lags exposure.** Unit 1 (a i m s t) gets two full weeks of daily 30-minute sessions. Sound Swap starts only at the end of week 1. Tier 1 pupils are expected to *recognise* Unit 1 code by the end of Unit 1, *read and write single words* by the end of Unit 2, and write Unit 1 in dictation only by the end of Unit 4 ([Sounds-Write, *Suggested implementation of the Initial Code*, 2020](https://www.ckschool.leics.sch.uk/attachments/documents.asp?id=108)). The school confirms "two weeks learning each group of sounds" (YR deck, p25).
4. **There are five lesson types, mixed three per session.** Word Building (L1), Symbol Search (L2: hear a sound, find its spelling), Sound Swap (L3), Word Reading (L4) and Dictation (L4a). A new unit is taught through L1, L2 and L4, while the previous unit is recycled through L3 and reading ([same planning doc](https://www.ckschool.leics.sch.uk/attachments/documents.asp?id=108); [Barley Hill summary](https://www.barley-hill.oxon.sch.uk/parents/phonics-sounds-write)). One S~W school's Unit 1 list is *mat sit sat Sam Tim it at am*, with the Sound Swap chain *mat → sat → sit → it → at → am* ([Easington S~W planning](http://easingtonprimary.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/EYFS-Sounds-Write-Phonics-Overview.pdf)).
5. **Start with two- and three-sound words.** "To begin with, we shall be working only with 2- and 3-sound words" ([S~W parent guide](https://www.yewtreeprimary.co.uk/attachments/documents.asp?id=726)).
6. **Scaffolds for children who can't blend yet** ([Sounds-Write blog, 2024](https://sounds-write.co.uk/mastering-phonemic-awareness-your-key-to-psc-success/)):
   - Start with continuant CVC words the child already knows orally.
   - Offer a choice of two pictured words that start with the same sound ("This word is 'map' or 'mug'. Say the sounds and listen for the word"). Widen to three or four choices later, then to minimal pairs such as cat and cot.
   - Say "I'll say the sounds, you listen for the word" while pointing.
   - If that fails, do a full model: "I'm going to say the sounds: /m/ /a/ /n/. I can hear 'man'. Did you hear 'man'? … Now you have a go."
   - Children should blend "after they have heard it modelled two or three times".
7. **Correct errors at the exact point where they happen, and don't give the word away.** "If this was 'big', this [point at n] would be 'g'. Is it? What is it? Yes, 'n'. Let's sound it out again" (YR deck p35). For an untaught spelling: "This is one sound. It's /k/. Say /k/ here" ([S~W manual extract](https://portal-api.sounds-write.co.uk/files/069381a8-8e69-41d6-bf73-e46b4fdc0d54/content?u=3z838tvqll7rtlvz61zyh7sm0xf7csgc)). For a mispronounced sound, the teacher points to her mouth and models the pure sound.
8. **Sounds~Write in nursery (age 3–4) keeps oral work brief.** The pilot of S~W's Early Years course found "limited value in doing phonemic awareness without letters". The course has "a bit of oral sound identification, segmenting and blending, but it moves very quickly to working with letters". Sounds are always in words, sometimes introduced with a toy or picture ([Sounds-Write, St Oswald's nursery](https://sounds-write.co.uk/success-story-st-oswalds-nursery-phonics/)).

### Early phonological awareness research (ages 3–5)

- **Order of development.** Children handle big units before small ones (words, then syllables, then onset/rime, then phonemes). They blend before they segment at the same level, and they detect "same or different" before they manipulate sounds ([Anthony et al. 2003, *RRQ*](https://doi.org/10.1598/rrq.38.4.3); [Anthony & Lonigan review](https://https-sage-cnpereading-com-443.webvpn1.xju.edu.cn/doi/10.1111/j.0963-7214.2005.00376.x)). Even 3½-year-olds can identify and blend units in an animated game, and **initial targets are easier than final ones** ([Puolakanaho et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222194030360050301)). Rhyme is a weak, late indicator ([Lonigan et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3238544/)), so skip rhyme games.
- **Phoneme identity works for 4-year-olds.** In Byrne & Fielding-Barnsley's *Sound Foundations* trials, preschoolers searched posters for things beginning (or ending) with a sound, in 12 sessions of 25–30 minutes. They made durable gains in phonemic awareness and decoding. The sounds trained were **/s/ /m/ /t/ /p/ and /a/**, almost exactly Sounds~Write Unit 1, with the letter displayed throughout. Initial position was learned much more readily than final ([Byrne & Fielding-Barnsley 1995](https://sci-hub.st/storage/zero/5915/6d006088da56ae1acb57ff7381fda31f/byrne1995.pdf)).
- **Teach phonemic awareness with letters, keep it focused, keep it short.** The National Reading Panel meta-analysis ([Ehri et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1598/rrq.36.3.2)) found phonemic awareness teaching was more effective:
  - **with letters** than without;
  - when it focused on **one or two skills** rather than many (transfer to reading was twice as big);
  - when it lasted **5–18 hours in total**. Longer programmes were less effective.
- **Phoneme awareness and letter–sound knowledge are the two causal foundations, and they develop together.** Letters make phonemes easier to notice ([Hulme et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611435921); [Castles, Rastle & Nation 2018](https://education.ufl.edu/patterson/files/2019/04/CastlesRastleNation2018.pdf)). The DfE says: "It seems easier for children to identify phonemes in words when they know how they correspond to letters… Phonics sessions might be only ten minutes long in the first few days" ([Reading Framework, section 3](https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/id/eprint/38964/2/Reading_framework_Teaching_the_foundations_of_literacy_-_Final_for_Publication_Section_3_Jan_22_update.pdf)).
- **The EEF says the same for ages 3–5.** Phonological awareness activities help before systematic phonics starts. Blending ("s-o-ck… what is it?") and knowing the start, middle and end of words are the key checks ([EEF *Preparing for Literacy*](https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/id/eprint/31819/1/Preparing_Literacy_Guidance_2018.pdf)). Support given directly by an adult may beat EdTech alone ([EEF Evidence Store](https://eef.dev/early-years/evidence-store/early-literacy)), which argues for the game modelling like a teacher rather than just quizzing.

### What good beginner apps do, and teaching method

- **GraphoGame** adapts so that about **80% of trials succeed**, gives a reward roughly every minute, and recommends **8–12 minute sessions** ([Jyväskylä overview](https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstreams/e288b6b1-d7e1-4c45-bdd5-5d55678f81da/download); [Frontiers 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00671/full)). A cautionary note: its UK RCT in Year 2 found no effect over business as usual ([EEF/NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/media/idlhbicn/graphogame_rime_evaluation_report_and_executive_summary.pdf)). Drilling without teaching isn't enough.
- **Gradual release ("I do, we do, you do")** is the standard explicit-instruction routine for preschool letter and phonemic awareness teaching. Keep it "short, frequent, playful", with cumulative review ([REL Southeast](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/rel/regions/southeast/pdf/se_Evidence_Based_Practices_for_Emergent_Literacy.pdf)). Spend most of the time in guided and independent practice, not teacher talk ([Pearson & Gallagher via Intervention in School and Clinic](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1323537.pdf)).
- **What this means for pre-readers:** instructions by voice only, a demonstration before every new mechanic, few choices at first (2 → 3 → 4), pictures that are always named aloud, errorless prompting that fades, and sessions around 10 minutes.

## (b) The progression for Bamboo Village (about 2 hours, from zero)

### Rules that apply to every mini-game

- **Voice-led, no text instructions.** Sensei says what to do, and a paw or hand animation shows it. Every picture is **named aloud when it appears** and again when tapped ("This is a fan"), because first-sound games fail if the child calls the picture something else.
- **I do → We do → You do in every mini-game:**
  - **I do (1 item):** Sensei does the whole item: taps, drags, says the sounds and reads. The child only watches. No input is accepted; a small "your turn" pulse follows.
  - **We do (2 items):** the correct answer glows from the start (item 1), then glows after a 2-second wait (item 2). Wrong targets are dimmed but tappable. After "Say it with me!" there is always a **1.5-second pause** so the child can answer aloud. The game can't hear them, but saying sounds aloud is central to Sounds~Write.
  - **You do (4–6 items):** no glow. Help appears only after an error or after 8 seconds of silence.
- **Errorless-first corrections:**
  - 1st error: go back to *listening* ("Listen… sssaaat. What do you hear *here*?", with the paw on the slot).
  - 2nd error: reduce the choice to the right answer plus one, and model it ("It's this one! This is /a/. Say /a/ as you put it here.").
  - The item is then **re-queued later in the round**, so the round always ends with an independent success.
  - Never show a fail screen.
- **Stars and mastery** count only **first-try independent (You do) items**. Errors during I do and We do never cost anything.
- **Move-on rule:** at least 80% first-try correct on the last 5 You do items, the same success rate GraphoGame targets. Below that, the stop replays automatically with one fewer choice and a different picture set, framed as "Let's practise that again!"
- **Warm-up:** every stop opens with 2 quick items from earlier stops (spaced retrieval).
- **Session length:** after about 10–12 minutes, Sensei suggests a rest (a "dojo nap"). Never force it.
- **Choices:** 2 in the first appearance of any mechanic, then 3, and 4 at most in Bamboo Village.

### Mini-game types

### When letters appear, and when 2-sound words come before 3-sound words

- **Letters appear in stop 2**, about 5 minutes in, and only as the reveal of a sound the child has just *heard in a word*. Never as a bare flashcard.
- Each new spelling is used in a real word **in the same stop** or the next.
- **2-sound words before 3-sound words.** *am* (two continuants, the easiest to blend) comes before *at* (ends in a stop), which comes before *it*. The first 3-sound word is *mat* (continuant start, has a picture), then *sat*, then *sit*.
- **Within Unit 1, spellings drip in** in the order that makes words soonest: **m, s → a → t → i**. Sounds~Write teaches all five over two weeks, and the game spreads them over about 45 minutes of play.

## (d) Sounds~Write rules to respect

1. **Sounds, not letter names.** No letter names anywhere (the school doesn't use them until late Reception, YR deck p41).
2. **Letters don't talk.** Spellings *spell* or *represent* sounds. Never say "says" or "makes" ([S~W parent guide](https://files.schudio.com/farcet-c-of-e-primary-school/files/documents/Sounds-Write-a-Guide-for-Reception-Parents.pdf); YR deck p20). Say "This is how we spell /a/", "Which of these is /a/?" and "That's a spelling of /i/".
3. **Say "spelling" (or "sound") rather than "letter"** for tiles, because *ff*, *sh* and *igh* are one tile, one sound. Use "two letters, one sound" and "three letters, one sound" when those arrive.
4. **Pure sounds, no added "uh".** Use "mmm", not "muh". The school's specific notes are w = "wwoo" and y = "yyee" (YR deck p19).
5. **No mnemonics, actions, songs or letter pictures.** Pictures are *words to listen to*, never keyword cards fixed to a letter. Use **different** pictures each time (sun, sock, sand…), and never put a picture on a tile or petal in the Initial Code games.
6. **Sounds first, in words.** Go from speech to print: hear the word, find the sound, then see its spelling. Don't teach sound–letter pairs in isolation.
7. **Continuant-first words.** Begin with /m/ /s/ /a/ /f/ /n/ starts, which are stretchable. Stop consonants (/t/ /p/ /k/) come later.
8. **Don't segment for the child when they are spelling.** Stretch the word and point to the slot. Saying /s/ /a/ /t/ out loud gives the answer away. That belongs to reading models ("I'll say the sounds, you listen for the word").
9. **"Say the sounds, read the word"** after every build. **"Say the sounds, write (build) the word"** while building. After the first sounding, say the sounds with no gap (YR deck p15).
10. **Correct at the exact place of the error.** "If this was *sit*, this would be /i/. Is it? What is it?" Then re-sound the whole word. Never just give the word.
11. **In the Initial Code, don't say "the easiest way to spell /a/".** That implies alternatives, which Sounds~Write introduces formally only in the Bridging Unit and Extended Code. Say "This is how we spell /a/". "One way to spell…" and "same sound, different spelling" belong from world 3 onward, as now.
12. **Special words** (a, is, the, I …) are taught whole: "This is *the*. Say *the*." (YR deck p22)
13. **Pace follows mastery, not the clock.** The school takes two weeks per unit. Gate each unit on the child's performance.

## (e) What the current game does that conflicts with good practice

1. **`Dojo` Learn teaches isolated pairs.** It plays "Listen… /a/ /a/. This is how we write it. Tap it and say it with me", then taps twice, for five spellings in about a minute. This is exactly the paired-associate teaching Sounds~Write avoids. It isn't set in a word, and tapping twice is neither practice nor a check.
2. **All five Unit 1 spellings arrive in one sitting** (`teach: ["a","i","m","s","t"]`), and Unit 2 arrives two levels later, inside the first ~20 minutes. The school spends four weeks on this.
3. **`Find` asks for recall immediately**, with 4 choices after one exposure per sound. For a true beginner that is guessing, and the misses feed `mistakes`, which lowers stars.
4. **`Build` has no worked example.** There is no I do: the first word goes straight to the child ("Build the word… mat").
   - The word is said at normal speed, not stretched, and nothing marks which slot to listen to.
   - `tileBank(..., 2)` always adds at least 2 distractors, so a beginner gets 4–5 tiles for a 2–3 sound word. Sounds~Write starts with exactly the needed cards.
   - `chooseWords` picks randomly among *at, am, it, sat, mat, sit* (weighted by newness), so there is no 2-sound-before-3-sound ordering.
5. **Help level 2 in `Build` segments for the child** ("Say the sounds… /s/ /a/ /t/"), which is the answer. Walker: "take care not to… do the segmenting for the children". It should stretch the word and point to the active slot instead.
6. **`correction()` gives the answer straight away** ("That's /i/. We need /a/.") instead of first sending the child back to listen ("Listen: s-aaa-t. What do you hear here?"), then offering the answer on a second miss.
7. **There are no beginner phonological games.** Nothing covers telling words apart, blending with pictures, first sounds or middle sounds. The child's only way to show blending is passive (`sayBlend` reads the word *to* them), and reading is first tested in a moving Ninja Run.
8. **Level 2 is a spelling Battle** straight after first exposure. Sounds~Write expects single-word reading and writing of Unit 1 only by the end of Unit 2. Battles should come after guided building and reading (stop 6 above).
9. **`Training` tests letter–sound knowledge before any teaching** ("Tap the tile that says /s/", then /m/, then /a/).
10. **Language that breaks Sounds~Write rules** (`src/content/lines.ts`):
    - `that_says` ("That says…"), `place_tap` ("Which one says…"), `tut_tile` / `tut_test` ("the tile that says…"): use "Which one is…" / "Find…".
    - `place_sound` ("Which spelling makes this sound?"): use "Which is the spelling of this sound?"
    - `same_sound_spelling` ("that spelling can make that sound too"): use "Yes, that's a spelling of … too. But in this word we spell it like this."
    - `help_tiles` ("Tap the letter tiles"): use "the sound tiles" or "the spellings".
11. **Stars count all mistakes**, including those made while learning. Only independent first tries should count.
12. **There is no session-length guidance.** A 3–4 year old can play Dojo, Battle, Dojo and Run back to back. A soft rest nudge at about 10–12 minutes follows GraphoGame and DfE guidance for the first days.
