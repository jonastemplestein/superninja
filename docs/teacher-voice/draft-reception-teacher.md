# Sensei's teacher voice: the complete script (Reception teacher's draft)

**27 September 2026, the teacher-voice workflow.** Written by a Reception teacher trained in Sounds~Write, as the script designer for the fix plan ([FIX_PLAN_PERF_SCRIPT_SOUNDS.md](../FIX_PLAN_PERF_SCRIPT_SOUNDS.md), not started yet). It answers Jonas, after playtesting the preschool levels with his 3- and 4-year-old:

> "I find that this extremely abbreviated way of talking, it doesn't help at all. Like teachers give, do like way better explanations, right? They say, so first, I'm going to show you how to do it. Are you ready? And this is how this game goes. I will show you this, and you will do that. Do you want to give it a go now? … Teachers explain what they're doing."

It is built on the three inputs in this folder: what a child hears today ([current.md](current.md), [current-transcript.md](current-transcript.md)), how good teachers introduce a game ([research.md](research.md)), and the frame / show / Ready? / your-turn mechanics with the ▶ and paw readiness answer ([mechanics.md](mechanics.md)). It keeps what [SCRIPT_STYLE.md](../SCRIPT_STYLE.md) and [SCRIPT_FIXES.md](../SCRIPT_FIXES.md) get right about repetition, dosage and praise, and replaces the parts that lean too far towards brevity. Every line is final wording for recording unless marked *(option)*. Nothing under `src/` was changed.

---

## 0. The approach, in short

1. **Every game is introduced the way I'd introduce it on the carpet:** its name, who does what ("I say a word, and you tap its picture."), a demonstration in the first person ("I'll go first. My word is sun. There it is!"), a real "Do you want to have a go?" answered with a tap, and then the child's turn. This happens the first time only. Replays get one reminder line ("Let's play Sound Swap.").
2. **Short, but whole.** Sentences are 4–10 words, with a subject and a verb. There are no slogans ("Ninja ears on!", "Ninjas read this way!", "Spell…") and no bare labels ("Let me show you!", "Now you try!", "Watch me first!"). Instructions end in a full stop, questions in a question mark, and only celebrations and surprises get "!", so the voice stops shouting.
3. **No turn of Sensei's runs past about 12 seconds before the child can do something.** I count a tap, an answer during a question, or saying a sound aloud with Sensei (Sounds~Write's "Everyone say that sound") as doing something. Where a frame and a demonstration together would run longer, the frame ends on a **Watch?** hold: "I'll do one first. Tap the arrow, and watch me." That is Jonas's "So first, I'm going to show you how to do it. Are you ready?", answered with the ▶ the child already knows.
4. **No bare "Listen…".** Sounds arrive with a sentence that says what is coming ("Here's the first new sound…" /b/), and "listen" always comes with what to listen for ("Listen to how sun and sock start.", "Let's listen to the word again, slowly…"). The Dojo no longer opens on a bare "Listen…" and an unexplained ear: it opens with what we're going to do today, and the petal is named and used ("This is its petal. Tap the petal to hear it again.").
5. **Every symbol is explained where it is first used:** the green arrow, the speaker, the rabbit and tortoise, the petal, the pockets, the paw, letters ("This is how we write…" /m/), Kai and Suki, the monster's bar, the tick in the story.
6. **The demonstration speaks as "I", never as the child's order.** "My word is sun." / "Your word is sock." "I take this one out, and put this one in." A question in a demonstration is Sensei thinking aloud ("Hmm…"), never one the paw then answers.
7. **Every game has a name** that Sensei says, so a later "Remember Sound Swap?" means something: Ninja Ears, Fast and Slow, Sound Hunt, Slow Words, Ninjas Read This Way, Which Row?, Big Words, Guess My Word, Sound Dots, First Sounds, Ninja Eyes, Middle Sounds, Word Building, Who Read It Right?, New Sounds, Monster Battle, Boss Battle, Gem Battle, Sound Swap, Sorting, Ninja Run and Story Time.
8. **Praise is about the work, and rare.** The modelled answer is the feedback ("Mop starts with…" /m/). Specific lines ("You listened right to the start.", "You said that sound really well.") replace most "Amazing!"s. Trait praise ("You're a ninja master!", "What a clever ninja.") goes.
9. **The cost is paid for by cutting repeats, not by dropping the frame.** First meetings get longer by about one frame and one or two taps each. Repeat plays get shorter than today, because the frame shrinks to one line and the labels, stems and stacked praise go.

## 1. How Sensei talks now

### 1.1 The shape of a first meeting

| Step | What happens | Line shape | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Name and frame** | the board arrives | "This game is called X. I …, and you …." Present tense, "I" for Sensei, "you" for the child, in the order it happens | ≤ 7 s |
| **Watch?** *(only when frame + show would run past 12 s)* | ▶ pops in; the board stays still | "I'll do one first. Tap the arrow, and watch me." | ≤ 4 s + tap |
| **Show** | the paw, the ninja and the cards do one item | first person, what I'm doing and why: "My word is sun. There it is!" | ≤ 12 s, or split by a spoken response ("Say it with me…" /m/) |
| **Ready?** | ▶ and the paw pop in; the ninja faces ▶ in its ready stance | "Do you want to have a go now?" The first time ever, the arrow is named; the next two times, both answers (§2.1) | ≤ 3 s (the first three: ≤ 7 s) + tap |
| **Your turn** | the ninja bows; the turn's view | the question, as "your" version of the demo: "Your word is sock. Can you find the sock?" | as today |
| **Feedback** | the answer lands | the modelled answer; a praise line at most every second right answer | ≤ 3 s |
| **Wrap-up** | the game or level ends | what the child did, and what comes next | ≤ 5 s |

After a turn has started, nothing changes from today's rules: eager answers count during a question, explanations are protected, and the idle ladder and Help work as in NAVIGATION.md §3.2. The only change there is that re-asks are *rephrased*, not repeated (§2.7).

### 1.2 The four forms (from mechanics.md §3.2)

| Form | When | What plays |
|---|---|---|
| **full** | the first time this profile meets the game | name and frame · (Watch?) · show · Ready? · turn |
| **recap** | the first play in a later session while the game has had fewer than two full tellings, after 21 days away, or after the child struggled last time | "Remember X? …" (one line) · show · Ready? (short) · turn |
| **short** | the first play in a session after that | one line that names the game, then the turn |
| **none** | later plays in the same session | the question only |

An I do that teaches a new spelling or sound (First Sounds, Middle Sounds, Word Building, New Sounds) plays on every form, because it teaches content, not the game. It always ends on a short Ready? (§2.1), because an I do that runs straight into the next item's naming and question is a 15–20 s turn.

### 1.3 Words and punctuation

- **Sentences:** 4–10 words, one idea each, at most two steps in one instruction. A list of pictures is one clip ("Here's the sun, a sock and a cat.") with word timings for the spotlights.
- **Full stops** for frames, instructions and demonstrations; **question marks** for real questions, which always have a tap or a spoken answer; **"!"** only for celebrations and surprises ("Fish dog!", "Hooray! The monster ran away!"). Lines kept from today whose only change is punctuation are marked *re-record* (for example `nav_ready` "Tap the green arrow when you're ready.").
- **Sounds~Write words, exactly:** pure sounds; never letter names; spellings never "say" or "make" sounds; "This is how we write…" /m/ (the Early Years wording) in the first-sound and find games, "spell" from the first battle on, where it is explained ("That's how we spell a word."); "It's two letters, but it's one sound."; "Say the sounds and read the word."; "If you say the sounds, you can hear the word."; "Good, you said that sound really well."
- **One child, not a class:** "you", never "everyone".
- **Grown-ups are addressed as grown-ups** ("Grown-ups: …"), so the child knows that line isn't theirs.

### 1.5 How to read the tables

- **id:** `tv_…` is a new line to record. A plain id is today's line, kept as it is. *re-record* means same words, calm punctuation. `{w}` is a picture word, `{p}` a sound, and a family such as `fs_{w}` means today's per-word lines.
- **when:** a row starting with **↳** is a branch (wrong answer, idle, Help), not part of the main line of play.
- **Sensei says:** text in quotes is **one recorded clip**, a whole sentence or two. `/m/` is a pure-sound clip; it only ever comes at the end of a lead-in recorded to end suspended on "…", or alone. `[map]` is a word clip, `[map, slowly]` the stretched word, `[sun, held]` the held first sound. `(1 s)` is a silent beat that the timing counts.
- **the child does:** "—" means the child watches and listens. Anything else is an action: a tap, or saying a sound or word aloud with Sensei (followed by 1.5 s of quiet for it).
- **Timing** (under each table) is computed from the tables by `playtest/runs/teacher-voice/script-runs.py`. It uses Sensei's recorded pace of about 2.7 words a second, 0.35 s between clips, 0.6 s for a pure sound, 0.7 s for a word, 1.7 s for a stretched word and 1.1 s for a held first sound. "Longest turn" is the longest stretch of Sensei's talk before the child can act. The child can act from the *start* of a question or invitation, because answers during it count.

### 2.5 Praise

- After a right answer, the modelled answer is the feedback: "Mop starts with…" /m/, the read-back, the card's own word.

- Specific beats generic. Generic words are for surprise, not routine.

### 6.2 What it costs

- **First meetings are longer, and the child is busier.** Each full form adds one frame (3–7 s) and one or two taps (Ready?, and sometimes Watch?), and most add a spoken response ("Say it with me…"). W1 goes from about 98 s to about 105 s for the bot (112 s with the ◇ rabbit), with eight taps and a spoken sound where today there are five taps and a spoken sound. The first five minutes grow by about 20 s, all of it the child's own taps and turns (mechanics.md §5.4's budget of 5:15 covers it).
- **Repeat plays are shorter than today.** The short form is one line in place of today's labels, repeated stems and stacked praise. For example, w1-3 opens with "Let's play First Sounds again." instead of "Every word starts with a sound. Let's listen for the very first sound! Watch me first!".
- **Recording:** 302 new lines (§5.1, including the per-game map previews and the fixed I do pairs), about 30 re-records (§5.2) and about 60 retirements (§5.3). About 50 of the new lines are recap and short forms and map previews, which a child hears only on later plays or once.

### 6.3 Decisions I made without asking (for integration to log in docs/DECISIONS.md)

1. **A spoken response counts as the child doing something.** "Say it with me…" + a sound, then 1.5 s of quiet, breaks a run of talk. It is Sounds~Write's own main response ("Everyone say that sound"), research.md §3 counts it, and it keeps first meetings to one or two extra taps.
2. **The Watch? hold** (▶ before a show) is used only where frame + show would run past 12 s: Slow Words, Guess My Word, Word Building (and its recap form), Monster Battle, Sound Swap and Sorting. It is Jonas's own "So first, I'm going to show you how to do it. Are you ready?". The paw stays "show me *again*".
3. **Every I do ends on a short Ready?,** on every form where it plays, not only on full and recap forms as mechanics.md §6.2 had it. Without it, an I do runs straight into the we do's naming and question, a 15–20 s turn.
4. **The first Ready? names only the arrow; the second and third teach the paw.** W1's first game is the tightest budget, and the paw means most after a longer show (Fast and Slow). The ear is not brought back as an "ears ready" button (research.md's option): ▶ stays the one "go on" control (mechanics.md §4).
5. **The map says what a new game is** (`tv_map_next_{game}`) before its stone, only for games the child has never played. The stone tap is the child's "yes", and the level can start with its rules. SF C6's quiet map is otherwise unchanged.
6. **The I do pairs in First Sounds and Middle Sounds are fixed per level** (map/hat, bed/sock, cat/apple, jam/tent, nut/hat, bus/pig, pan/pin, map/mop), so each pair is named in one clip. The we do and you do pictures stay random.
7. **"Write" before "spell".** The Early games use the Early Years wording "This is how we write…" /m/. "Spell" is introduced at the first battle ("That's how we spell a word."). The World Flower keeps the official "This is the way we spell it in mat.", with the sound said just before it (decision 14). A child will hear both verbs, as they do at school.
8. **Game names** (§0.7). Two are new: **Ninja Eyes** (find how we write a sound; it pairs with Ninja Ears) and **Which Row?**.
9. **The first Dojo Learn has a real petal turn:** "This is its petal. Tap the petal to hear it again." It waits for the tap with the usual idle ladder, once per save. That is the answer to "this ear appears": the thing that appears is named, and used.
10. **The gem's first-fill explanation moves to the reward** instead of interrupting w1-4's we do as a held step.
11. **The dojo welcome ends on a held step** that names the first game ("It's called Ninja Ears. Tap the arrow, and let's play.") instead of moving on by itself.
12. **The speaker in the welcome gets something to repeat** (the ninja's "Hi-ya!"), instead of SF C19's sun, because nothing else is on that screen.
13. **Trait and inflated praise go** ("You're a ninja master!", "What a clever ninja", everyday "Amazing!"); the Sounds~Write praise "Good, you said that sound really well." comes in.
14. **No sound in the middle of a sentence, even in the official phrase.** Sounds~Write's "This is the way we spell /m/ in mat." becomes the sound in its own slot, then a whole sentence: "Here's the sound…" /m/ · "This is the way we spell it in mat." That needs a generated family, `tg_{g}_{p}_way`, beside today's `tg_{g}_{p}_in` (SF C1 and C3 change accordingly). It keeps the brief's rule that a pure sound only ends a lead-in or stands alone.
15. **Superseded:** FIRST_MINUTES §3 rule 3 ("no mode announcements"), SCRIPT_STYLE §5.1's "Listen…" before every stretched word, and the SCRIPT_STYLE §5 idea of fading to a bare stimulus for ages 3–4. SCRIPT_STYLE's rules on repetition, dosage and praise stay.
