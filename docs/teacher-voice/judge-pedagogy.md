# Judge: pedagogy (Sounds~Write, mechanics, recordability)

**27 September 2026, teacher-voice workflow, the pedagogy judge.** It compares the three script drafts against one lens and nothing else:
- **Sounds~Write fidelity:** pure sounds, no letter names, spellings never "say" or "make" sounds, I do / we do / you do, and the programme's own teacher language (assets-src/sw-sources/research/sections/teacher-language.md and nursery-precode.md).
- **Mechanics:** does every line have a trigger that exists in [mechanics.md](mechanics.md) (frame, show, Ready? after the show, turn; the four forms; `holdReady`, the paw, the board tap), or does it need a new one?
- **Recordability:** can it be recorded as whole sentences, with a sound or word only at the end of a lead-in or in its own slot?

It does not judge warmth, comedy or voice; the other judges do. **R** is [draft-reception-teacher.md](draft-reception-teacher.md), **C** is [draft-cbeebies-writer.md](draft-cbeebies-writer.md) and **E** is [draft-early-years-specialist.md](draft-early-years-specialist.md). Scores are 1–10 on this lens only: roughly half Sounds~Write, a third mechanics, the rest recordability. A 9 means "ship it with small edits". A 5 means a structural problem.

## 1. Cross-cutting findings (the harsh part)

1. **E's Ready? is in the wrong place.** E puts ▶ after the frame and before the demo (E §4.1), where mechanics §3.1 and decision 2 put it after the show.
   - The child taps "I'm ready", hears "Here we go.", and then sits through 7–10 s of naming and demo. So ▶ means "ready to watch", and the real hand-over collapses to the ritual label "Now it's your turn." That is the "Now you try!" pattern Jonas complained about.
   - It also removes the paw as the second answer to the hold (mechanics §4.2), so "Show me again" is only discovered at the 8 s idle point.
   - E's budget argument (a post-show hold reaches 15–23 s) is real, but R and C both solve it with join-ins, so the move isn't needed.
   - **Fix:** move `tv_rdy_q` after the show, and word it as the hand-over ("Now you find the other two. Ready?").

2. **R taps ▶ too often, for nothing.** R adds a Watch? hold ("I'll do one first. Tap the arrow, and watch me.") before the show in six games: Slow Words, Guess My Word, Word Building, Monster Battle, Sound Swap and Sorting. Its decision 3 also puts a Ready? after every I do on every form, including short forms.
   - Across Bamboo Village that is about a dozen ▶ taps that mechanics doesn't have. It contradicts mechanics decision 2 (Jonas's first "Are you ready?" gets no tap) and §6.2 ("the second sound's I do stays … with no R").
   - Each tap is a tap with no choice in it. Round 13's "too long and boring" is exactly where this goes.
   - **Fix:** replace every Watch? with a join-in that means something: C's word-card tap in Word Building, C's child-reads-the-start-word in Sound Swap, and "Say it with me…" elsewhere. Keep the I do Ready? on full and recap forms only.

3. **C needs about ten new mechanics** that mechanics.md doesn't have. C lists them honestly in §4.4, but each is lane work before a line can play:
   - tappable cards in the W1 notice, and the removal of its `W1:7` hold;
   - the petal as a waited-for join-in: W1, W3, w1-2, w1-7, four times in w2-1, Reward 2 and the World Flower;
   - "I'll start, you finish" tiles, live halfway through Sensei's demo;
   - a Ready-to-watch hold in Sound Swap, and swap tiles as sound buttons;
   - chests that open on the child's taps;
   - the rabbit made compulsory (a governor change);
   - the letter written on the we do instead of the I do.

   The joins are good pedagogy: every one is the child saying a sound or hearing a word, not an empty tap. But the fix plan has to budget them. **C's first battle has no demo (D12), which breaks mechanics §6.4.** Use R's battle show.

4. **Bare one-word barks survive in all three.** mechanics §7.3 diagnoses "Listen…" as a single stressed syllable at a sentence's loudness. The same fault is still here:
   - C's `tv_watch` "Watch." (Pocket Hunt, W1 and W3);
   - R's `tv_dj_watch_short` "Watch." (w2-1) and `tv_ms_pair_*` "Watch. Here's a pan and a pin.";
   - "Your turn." as the Dojo series fade: C's `tv_your_turn`, E's `tv_turn_short`;
   - E's "And once more." and "Last one.".

   **Fix:** no clip under three words except praise and celebrations. Extend F4's `bare-listen` check to any one-word instruction.

5. **R's recaps are rhetorical questions.** "Remember Sound Swap? …", "Remember First Sounds? …" and about 20 more. That breaks R's own §1.3 rule ("question marks for real questions, which always have a tap or a spoken answer"). **Fix:** use C's "It's Sound Swap again. We change one sound, to make a new word." or E's "You know Sound Swap."

6. **The Find question.** Sounds~Write's Lesson 1 asks "which of these is the way we write /s/". C has it verbatim ("Which of these is the way we write… /m/") and E nearly ("Which one is the way we write…"). R's rotation, "Can you find…" / "Where's…" / "Now find…", asks the child to find a *sound*, which can't be seen. Use C's stems.

7. **Sound Swap, Sounds~Write's rule.** The child must say the sounds and read the start word before any swap (Lesson 3).
   - C does it: the child taps and says them.
   - R does it together with Sensei.
   - E skips it: Sensei reads "This is mat. I want to change it to sat." for the child.

8. **SOUND_DISPLAY A12 (a petal picture used as an answer card).** R's fixed I do pair `tv_fsd_pair_cat_apple` and E's w1-3 demo ("This is an apple.") put the apple card beside the /a/ petal, whose picture is an apple, so the child matches two pictures instead of listening. Only C caught it, and moved its demo to the ant.

9. **The /k/ examples in w2-1.** R reuses `tp_k_hear` "You can hear it in cat, king and duck." straight before "This is how we write…" /k/ shows < c >. Two of the three examples are spelt < k > and < ck >. For the first Dojo, use < c > words (cat, cup, cot). Or, for a sound with more spellings to come, say "This is one way we write…", as Sounds~Write's founder does: the way we spell /k/ *in this word*.

10. **Recording defects.**
    - R's line table has `tv_fsd_pair_jam_tent` = "Now let's play Ninja Eyes." (§5.1), a copy error that would be recorded as written.
    - R's `tv_listen_again_row` "That row is dog… cat. Let's listen again." exists for one pair only, and W4's three-picture rows have no version.
    - R's sort uses the fragments `st_like_this_in` "…like this, in…" [kit], which is a sentence spliced around a word.
    - C's `tv_swap_in` "…and in goes… /s/" starts mid-sentence. Make it "And in goes… /s/".
    - E flags its one mid-sentence sound (the concept-4 contrast, a SCRIPT_FIXES C20 legacy). R and C say nothing about it, so it survives in all three.

11. **Untrue lines** (Sounds~Write asks for "honest, accurate" language):
    - R's `tv_tut_gong` "Every lesson starts with the gong." It doesn't: the gong is the title tap and Ninja Run's finish.
    - R's `tv_practise_again` "Let's play it again, with new pictures." The `needsRepeat` repeat replays the same W4.
    - C's `tv_find_frame` "In this game, I say a picture, and you find it." She says a word (mechanics' example has the same slip).
    - E's `tv_spd_rabbit` "Rabbits are fast." It is a fact about rabbits, not what the button does. R's "It says words fast." is the information the child needs.

12. **Praise.** R is closest to Sounds~Write's own ("Good, you said that sound really well.", "Lovely.", "Well done."), and it retires "Amazing!", "Fantastic!", "Smashing!" and "Ace!" from routine use.
    - C keeps "Smashing!" and "Ace!" in the rotation, and "Sorted! What a clever ninja.", against its own rule 9.
    - E keeps `streak_10` "Amazing! You're a ninja master!".

## 2. Section by section

Each section gives the scores, the best and worst lines (with the draft and id), and concrete fixes.

### 1. Film, Choose, opt-in: R 7 · C 8 · E 7 → **C**
- **Best:** C `tv_opt_why` "Before we start, I want to choose the right games for you." It gives the reason before the question.
- **Worst:** R `tv_opt_notyet` "Not yet? Then tap the teddy." A 3-year-old hears the answer before the question has landed. That is today's telegraphese, re-recorded.
- **Fixes:**
  - C: use E's wording, "If you don't go yet, tap the teddy." ("If not yet…" is harder to parse).
  - Take R's `tv_choose_1` "Hello! I'm Sensei Maple, and I'm going to be your teacher." It names the teacher role Jonas is asking for.
  - E doesn't cover the film or Choose at all.

### 2. Dojo welcome: R 6 · C 8 · E 8 → **E**
- **Best:** E `tv_tut_next_game` "Next, we're going to play a listening game.", used as what the speaker says again. The speaker replays a real instruction, which is its job, and it bridges to W1 with no new art.
- **Worst:** R `tv_tut_gong` "Every lesson starts with the gong. Can you tap it?" It is untrue (§1.11). R's "Here's my ninja shout. Hi-ya!" also needs a new kick timed to the speaker.
- **Fixes:**
  - E: end on a held ▶ that names the game (C `tv_first_game` "It's a listening game, called Ninja Ears. Tap the green arrow when you're ready."), instead of "(W1 starts)".
  - Keep E's star lines ("That's your first star. Two more to go.").

### 3. W1 find the picture: R 9 · C 8 · E 6 → **R**
- **Best:** R `tv_w1_show` "I'll go first. My word is sun. There it is!" with `tv_w1_turn` "Your word is sock. Can you find the sock?" The I do and the you do share one sentence shape, which is what the Sounds~Write model does ("I'm going to say the sounds… Now you have a go").
- **Worst:** E `tv_rdy_play` "Are you ready to play? Tap the green arrow." then `tv_rdy_go` "Here we go.", followed by 9.5 s of naming and demo before the child can act (§1.1). Runner-up: C's "I say a picture".
- **Fixes:**
  - R: the first Ready? names only ▶ (the paw comes at the second). That breaks mechanics §4.2's long form, so log it as a decision. C made the same call.
  - `tv_w1_names` ("Here's the sun, a sock and a cat.") is one clip, so it needs Whisper word timings for the spotlights.

### 4. W1 fast and slow: R 8 · C 9 · E 7 → **C**
- **Best:** C `fm_hear_sounds` ↻ "When I say a word slowly, I can hear the sounds that make up the word." It is Sounds~Write Lesson 1's set-up phrase, said after the child has done it.
- **Worst:** E `tv_spd_rabbit` "This is my rabbit. Rabbits are fast." (§1.11), then a Ready? before the child has seen either button work.
- **Fixes:**
  - C's word-independent `tv_ts_fast` / `tv_ts_slow` ("The rabbit says words fast…" [w]) is the right recording design. It retires the missing `fm_fast_mug` for good, whereas R's `tv_fs_rabbit_mug` bakes the word in.
  - Trim C `tv_ready_paw` from four instructions to "Ready? Tap the arrow. To see it again, tap my paw."
  - Log C's D6 (the rabbit becomes compulsory), or keep it ◇.

### 5. W1 the notice: R 8 · C 8 · E 7 → **R**
- **Best:** R `tv_nt_listen` "Listen to how sun and sock start.", which is "listen" with its object. Also C `tv_ears_on` "Look, your ninja has its ninja ears on. That means listening really carefully.", the only line in any draft that explains "ninja ears".
- **Worst:** C `tv_tap_the_<w>` "Tap the sun." / `tv_now_tap_<w>` "Now tap the sock." These are bare commands in a beat whose job is noticing, and they need tappable cards that don't exist in this beat.
- **Fixes:**
  - R: put C's `tv_ears_on` before `tv_nt_listen`.
  - R keeps the `W1:7` Next hold with words (`tv_sh_next`), as mechanics §6.1 says. Good.
  - E's `tv_say_with_me` "Say it with me. /s/" should be recorded suspended ("Say it with me…"), so the sound lands as its object.

### 6. W1 pockets: R 8 · C 6 · E 8 → **R**

- **Fixes:**

Each graft takes the section winner as its base.

**Structure (for the whole merge)**
1. **Ready? comes after the show, on full and recap forms only** (mechanics §3). This throws out E's pre-show hold, R's Watch? holds and R's decision 3 (a Ready? after the I do on short forms).
