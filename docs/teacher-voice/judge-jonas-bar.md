# Judge: does it clear Jonas's bar?

**27 September 2026, teacher-voice workflow, one of the judges.** I read the three drafts in full ([draft-reception-teacher.md](draft-reception-teacher.md) "RT", [draft-cbeebies-writer.md](draft-cbeebies-writer.md) "CB", [draft-early-years-specialist.md](draft-early-years-specialist.md) "EYS"), alongside [current.md](current.md) and [mechanics.md](mechanics.md). No game code was touched and nothing was built: this is a reading of the words.

**My one lens.** Jonas, after playtesting with his 3- and 4-year-old:

> "They say, so first, I'm going to show you how to do it. Are you ready? And this is how this game goes. I will show you this, and you will do that. Do you want to give it a go now? … Teachers explain what they're doing."

So, for every line: does it sound like a warm teacher explaining what she's doing? Is anything still terse, barked or unexplained? Would Jonas, reading the transcript, be delighted? Round 13 ("too long and boring") is the brake: more words only count if they explain something.

## 1. How I judged

**Six checks per line and per section:**
1. **Frame:** does the game say what it is and who does what ("I'll…, then you…") before anything happens?
2. **Narrated demo:** first person, thinking aloud, and ideally *why* ("…so I tap it")? Never the child's command during Sensei's demo?
3. **Ready:** a real, whole question the child answers with a tap ("Are you ready?", "Do you want to have a go now?"), not a clipped "Ready?"
4. **Handover:** a clear "now you", in a whole sentence, then quiet?
5. **No barks:** no one-word orders, no slogans, no "!" on instructions, and no bare "Listen"?
6. **Delight:** is there a line Jonas would read out to someone?

**The read-aloud test.** These are the two moments Jonas played, composed verbatim from each draft's tables (▶ is the child's tap).

## 3. Why this ranking

- **CB first.** It is the only draft where every headline moment has a line a parent would repeat:
  - "Are you ready too?";
  - "Hmm, let me listen… So I'll tap it.";
  - "So out goes… and in goes…";
  - "Get your ninja ears ready. Here it comes…";
  - "I'll read some pages to you, and you'll read some pages to me.";
  - "Don't worry, ninja. You know what to do.".

  It also wins the two moments Jonas played. Its sins are mechanical and easy to fix: bare "Watch." and "Your turn.", "Now you. Ready?", the shouted game-name openers, a Pocket Hunt and a Slow Words demo that don't say why, and no transitions.
- **EYS second.** It is the most faithful to Jonas's order and the best at *explaining why*: "So I tap this row", "So it goes in a pocket", "If you say the sounds, you can hear the word". Its opt-in, correction ladder and transitions are the best of the three. But it replaces a real question with a ritual fragment, "Ready? Tap the green arrow.", in every game, and the transcript will show it about 16 times before w2-1. That is exactly "this extremely abbreviated way of talking", only now it's consistent. Its full stops on "You found the sock." and "Oh no." will read flat. And it has no post-demo "Do you want to have a go now?".
- **RT third.** It is the most natural Reception-teacher register, and its Ready lines are whole questions ("Do you want to have a go now?", "Now you do one. Are you ready?"). But its signature move, the Watch? hold ("I'll do one first. Tap the arrow, and watch me."), turns Jonas's warm "Are you ready?" into an interface instruction and doubles the arrow taps in six games. It also keeps a few old barks: "Watch.", "Not yet? Then tap the teddy.", "Look! This is how we write…".

## 6. For the merge: decisions my lens settles

1. **The post-demo Ready is a whole question, answered with ▶:** "Do you want to have a go now?" Its variants say what's next ("Now you do one. Are you ready?"). No clipped "Ready?" anywhere.
2. **The pre-demo "Are you ready?" is spoken, with no tap** ("I'll go first. Are you ready? Watch my paw."), except where frame and demo run past 12 s. There it's CB's "Are you ready to watch? Tap the arrow."
3. **Every demo says why, not just what:** "…So I tap it." / "…So it goes in a pocket." Where Sensei would think, she says "Hmm, let me listen." The paw never answers a question Sensei asked the child.
4. **No one-word or two-word instruction clips.** "Watch.", "Your turn.", "Last one." become whole sentences ("Watch my paw.", "Now you tap it, and say it.", "This is the last one.").
5. **A game's name is said inside a sentence** ("This game is called…", "Let's play…"), never shouted as a one-word opener.
6. **"!" belongs on celebrations and surprises only.** Frames, instructions and questions end calm.
7. **"Remember X?" is allowed when the answer follows at once.**
8. **Games link to each other.** A level with two games says "Next, we're going to…" between them, and a game ends by saying it has ended.
