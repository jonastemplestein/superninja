# Sounds~Write in Years 1 and 2: the middle and end of the progression

Research for the midgame and endgame (27 September 2026). Written for the agents redesigning the game, the marketing site and the videos, in answer to Jonas: "are these games really good to learn how to spell magician and optician and division and multiplication and all the shuns … I feel like the middle and end game isn't really well thought through."

**Scope.** Everything a child meets after the Initial Code: the 49 Extended Code units, the polysyllabic strand (how Sounds~Write teaches a word like *multiplication*), "shun" and the other special endings, schwa, prefixes and suffixes, spellings with more than one sound, high-frequency words, what the National Curriculum and the Phonics Screening Check expect at the end of Year 1 and Year 2, the skills involved and the typical errors. Section 9 turns it into recommendations for the game and for honest marketing claims.

**How to read it.** Facts carry a source key (list at the end). "Official" means a Sounds-Write Ltd document; "school" means a school's own document (Freshford is Jonas's children's school); "ours" means our inference or our application of the rules. This builds on `docs/SOUNDS_WRITE_MODEL.md` (the model and the ids) and `assets-src/sw-sources/research/DOSSIER.md` (the compendium, cited as DOSSIER §x); it does not repeat their detail where a pointer will do.

**Copyright.** Sounds~Write materials are quoted a phrase or a sentence at a time. Example words are single words picked from the official lists, never whole lists; the full lists stay in the git-ignored `assets-src/sw-sources/`. The National Curriculum is Crown copyright under the Open Government Licence.

**Notation.** /ae/ is a sound, < ai > is a spelling. A middle dot marks a syllable break in tables (sun·set); Sounds~Write writes sun|set. Sounds are given per syllable in the "spelling voice", e.g. mul·ti·ply = /m u l/ /t i/ /p l ie/. Y1 = Year 1 (age 5–6), Y2 = Year 2 (age 6–7).

## 0. The short version

1. **Year 1 is Extended Code Units 1–26; Year 2 is Units 27–49.** About two weeks per sound unit; eleven "spelling units" run inside the sound units. The targets are "Unit 26 by the end of Year 1" and "Unit 49 by the end of Year 2" [HB26]. After that, Year 3 reviews the whole Extended Code one sound a week; there is no Unit 50 [HB26; S&S-EC].
2. **Long words are a separate strand that starts in Year 1**, at about the second week of Unit 4 /oe/ (about week 8 of Year 1 in the official example plan), and runs to Year 6 [HB26; PSW-Intro]. It uses its own lessons (11–15), starts with Initial Code words (*sunset*, *dentist*, *fantastic*), then works back through the Extended Code **4–7 units behind** the main unit. By the end of Year 1 the official checks expect words like *nightmare*, *northern*, *award*; by the end of Year 2, *mechanic*, *character*, *chemistry*, *scholar* [Checks26].
3. **The routine for any long word is the same for a six-year-old and a ten-year-old:** say the word, find the syllables (one line per syllable), build or write each syllable sound by sound, read each syllable, then read the whole word; then write it again without gaps. The key line is "Let's say the word very precisely in its syllables" [TopTipsPSW; L11demo]. Section 3.2 has the exact lines.
4. **Jonas's words are in Sounds~Write's own teaching cards.** The official "Suffixes" card set lists *magician*, *optician*, *division*, *multiplication*, *musician*, *technician*, *television*, *location* and more [Doc07]. So yes: Sounds~Write teaches them, with the same routine.
5. **But they are late Year 2 at the earliest, and properly Year 3.** The National Curriculum puts *-tion* in Year 2 (station, fiction, motion, section) and *-cian*, *-sion*, *-ssion* in Years 3–4 (magician, division, permission) [NC-A1]. None of the official Sounds~Write progress checks up to the end of Year 2 tests a "shun" word [Checks26]. Freshford teaches "Suffixes: shun shul" in the summer term of Year 2 [Freshford-prog]. Honest framing: **a Year 2 stretch that school formally tests in Year 3**. *Multiply* itself is an official Year 2 spring word (check 26).
6. **How Sounds~Write codes "shun":** three sounds, /sh/ + schwa + /n/. In *station* the /sh/ is spelled < ti >, in *magician* < ci >, in *permission* < ssi >, in *tension* < si >; in *division* the < si > is /zh/. The lexicon: < ti > "only represents the sound 'sh' in three English suffixes", -tion, -tious and -tial [Lexicon]. Sounds~Write calls these endings "special endings" and gives them no unit number [Stat25].
7. **What gets harder in the middle and end game is not just "new sounds".** Five things grow together through Years 1–2: more spellings per sound (4 at first, up to 12 for /or/), spellings that stand for several sounds, longer words (2 → 5 syllables), weak syllables and schwa, and common endings. Each land's content should grow on all five axes, not only on the sound list (section 9.1).
8. **Bosses.** Sounds~Write's natural "boss" is the progress check, every 6–8 weeks, chosen by the last unit taught: word reading, word dictation and one dictated sentence, with polysyllabic words from the fourth week of Year 1 onwards [Checks26; ChecksFAQ]. A boss built on the check's rules gets harder by itself: the panda is the boss of the first two units; the last Year 2 boss would read *mechanic* and *chemistry* (section 9.2).
9. **Content problems found in the game** (section 9.4): the polysyllabic files have syllables but no sounds, so Lesson 11 can't be run at sound level; many syllable splits differ from Sounds~Write's own printed splits, and five split one spelling across two syllables (bot|tle, hap|pi|ly); EC18 has none of its own spellings (< al el il ol >: pencil, medal, camel); there is almost no "shun" content (location, musician, multiplication only); the /sh/, /ch/ and schwa petals miss < si >, < t > (picture) and several schwa spellings.

Sounds~Write does not expect new spellings in writing straight away: "we expect the child to remember them and be able to use them in their writing immediately. We don't!" (founder, DOSSIER §9.6). Move on "when 75-80% of your class has achieved 75-80% proficiency" [DOSSIER §13.3].

### 1.3 What is new after Reception

- **Concepts 3 and 4 become formal:** "The same sound can be spelled in more than one way" and "Many spellings can represent more than one sound"; concept 2 grows to "1, 2, 3 or 4 letters" (< eigh >, < ough >) [HB26].
- **New lessons:** 6 (one sound, different spellings: word puzzles), 7 (reading and writing), 8 (sound review), 9 (seek the sound), 10 (one spelling, different sounds), and 11–15 for long words. Lessons 3, 4 and 4a stay [HB26].
- **New language:** "It can be /a/, but in this word it's /ae/"; "This is a way of spelling /er/, but in this word we need this spelling"; "Say the word very precisely in its syllables"; "spelling voice" [DOSSIER §3; TTE-PSW].
- **Reading widens:** "From around midway through Year 1, most students will have mastered enough of the Extended Code to begin to move 'beyond decodables'" [PlanY2].

## 3. Long words: how Sounds~Write teaches polysyllabic words

### 3.1 When, and in what order

Official objectives: spell long words "by segmenting them first into syllables, and then each syllable, in turn, into sounds"; read them "by first blending sounds into syllables, and then syllables, in turn, into words"; know that some syllables are common prefixes and suffixes and that "some polysyllabic words contain schwas" [S&S-PSW]. Sounds~Write's reason for starting in Year 1: "80% of the words in the English language are polysyllabic" [HB26].

The order (Handbook, seven steps; official):

| Step | Content | Examples | Roughly when (ours, from the 2020 and 2023 example plans) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | two syllables, Initial Code, CVC·CVC and VC·CVC, many compounds | sun·set, up·set, co·mic, zig·zag, Bat·man | Y1, EC4 week 2 |
| 2 | harder Initial Code structures | in·sect, den·tist, desk·top, pro·ject | Y1, EC4–6 |
| 3 | two-letter spellings from the Initial Code | egg·shell, jack·pot, chop·stick, dish·cloth | Y1, EC5–7 |
| 4 | adjacent consonants in both syllables; three simple syllables | in·spect, splen·did, fan·tas·tic | Y1, EC6–8 |
| 5 | stress: find the strong and weak syllable, first without schwa, then with it | sun·lit; then co·llect, a·ddress, le·mon, ki·tchen | Y1, before EC8 |
| 6 | three syllables, Initial Code, may contain schwas | e·quip·ment, con·tra·dict | Y1 |
| 7 | the Extended Code again, in order, well behind the current unit; then suffixes and words of more than three syllables | day·break, pain·ting → Au·gust, wa·ter → mul·ti·ply → me·cha·nic | Y1 from about EC8, all of Y2 and beyond |

"We advise increasing structural complexity (the number of syllables and adjacent consonants) before adding less frequently encountered spellings of sounds and complexity of meaning" [HB26; PSW-Intro]. The official record sheet has five stages: two-, three-, four-, five-syllable words, then common suffixes [DOSSIER §10.4.4]. The official card sets reach five syllables (*mathematical*, *vocabulary*, *investigator*) [Doc07]; other official sources say up to six [DOSSIER §10.4.4].

**Exceptions.** New spellings are always introduced in one-syllable words, except Units 18 (< al el il ol >), 34 (< re ar our >) and 35 (< vv >), whose spellings only occur in longer words [HB26; ChecksFAQ].

### 3.2 The lessons and the teacher's words

| Lesson | Name | Who splits the word | How the child works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | Building Polysyllabic Words, Sound Level | teacher | builds and writes each syllable sound by sound |
| 12 | Reading Polysyllabic Words, Sound Level | teacher puts in the syllable lines | says the sounds, reads up to the line, says the syllable, then the word |
| 13 | Building Polysyllabic Words, Syllable Level | responsibility moves to the child | says and builds whole syllables |
| 14 | Reading Polysyllabic Words (syllable level) | child | reads syllable by syllable |
| 15 | Analysing Polysyllabic Words | child says it precisely in syllables | finds the hard spelling in each syllable, links it to easy words with the same spelling |

11/13 are the building pair and 12/14 the reading pair. The move from 11–12 to 13–14 is "not linear": go back to 11–12 for four- to six-syllable words, rare spellings, unfamiliar words and any child who needs it [PSW-Intro; Ep20]. A literacy specialist recommends Lesson 14 before the Phonics Screening Check as "perfect practice" [DOSSIER §10.3.1].

**Lesson 11, the official demonstration with 'window'** [L11demo; TopTipsPSW] (short lines, machine-transcribed and checked against the video frames):

1. Tiles for the word's sounds, jumbled, one sound per tile (w, i, n, d, ow). One line per syllable.
2. "When I say 'window', you can hear two syllables." (The teacher gives the count in Lesson 11.)
3. "What's the first syllable you hear when I say 'window'?" The child answers 'win' and builds it on the first line, saying the sounds.
4. "Now, let's say the sounds and read the syllable": /w/ /i/ /n/, 'win'.
5. "What's the second syllable in the word 'window'?" and the same again for 'dow'.
6. Read the word: "Now let's say the sounds and read each syllable. Now say the syllables and read the word." Gestures: point, point, point for sounds; slide for a syllable [TopTipsPSW].
7. Step 4, writing: "Now let's say the word very precisely in its syllables." "Now write the first syllable, win, and say all the sounds as you do." Then the second. Then "write the word without the gap between the syllables."

So Jonas's paraphrase ("Say the word. How many syllables? Say the first syllable. What's the first sound?") is right in spirit, with two corrections: in Lesson 11 the **teacher** says how many syllables and draws the lines; and the child is not asked for single sounds one by one, but builds the syllable saying its sounds (the Lesson 1 "What's the first sound you hear?" question returns only as help). In Lessons 13–14 the child finds the syllables.

**Lesson 12** [Doc47]: "you put in the line to separate the syllables, ask the student to say the sounds, read up to the line, and say the syllable". Worked example: /f/ /r/ /o/ /g/ 'frog', /m/ /a/ /n/ 'man', 'frog' 'man', 'frogman'. This is also where schwa is found: the vowel sounds different in the isolated syllable and in the whole word [PSW-Intro]. The founder reads *autobiographical* this way (au·to·bi·o·gra·phi·cal) with a Year 3–6 class [DOSSIER §10.3.4].

**Lesson 15** [L15]: choose a word the children can already read. "Begin by asking the students to say the word very precisely in its syllables." For each syllable: "Are there any sounds in [syllable] that you might find difficult to spell?" Underline that spelling; find easy words with the same spelling (for *happy*: silly, funny, daddy). Two misconception lines: "that's the same sound, but it is a different spelling" (monkey), and "that's the same spelling, but it represents a different sound in this word" (yes). Then rub the word out and write it again in syllables.

**Dictating a long word** (progress checks): "The word is trick. 'Sam played a trick on me.' Write the word trick, say the sounds as you do." Long words are given the same way. The spelling voice is only for the word on its own, never inside the sentence [Checks26; ChecksFAQ].

**Five rules for all long-word lessons** [TopTipsPSW]: stick to the script; gestures match words; pre-plan the syllable split and tiles ("ONE sound per letter tile!"); apply it in decodable text and dictation; "Don't forget to use Lesson 15." Cover upcoming syllables when children rush.

### 3.3 Where to split a word

- **Listen, don't look.** Split by the natural syllable break in speech, not by meaning; splits can depend on accent [Ep20].
- **Start a syllable with a consonant where possible.** A double-consonant spelling stays whole at the start of the next syllable: ru·bbish, co·ffee, ha·ppy, bo·ttle, ro·tten, to·mo·rrow; a single consonant starts the next syllable: ho·li·day, e·le·phant, ma·ga·zine, to·ge·ther, me·cha·nic [Ep20; Checks26].
- **Never cut a spelling in two.** One tile is one sound, so < ow >, < ch >, < tt > stay whole: pow·der, pow·er·ful, an·chor, bu·tter·fly [TopTipsPSW; Checks26].
- **No hard rules.** "there isn't a hard and fast rule"; either split of *chicken* is accepted in training footage [Ep20; DOSSIER §10.6].
- The official progress checks print about 300 split words; they are the best reference for the game's content (list in `research/official-pdfs/2026-08-Progress-checks-for-the-Extended-Code-2026.txt`).

### 3.4 Stress, schwa and the spelling voice

- **Schwa** is "a weak vowel sound that is not spelled as it sounds in normal speech" [Stat25]. It is the commonest vowel in English and causes spelling problems, not reading problems [Lexicon].
- **Stress first.** Children learn to hear which syllable is louder, first in words without schwa: "Is it SUNlit, or sunLIT? Is it batMAN or BATman?" [Doc47; PSW-Intro].
- **Three two-syllable patterns** to meet with Initial Code words: no schwa (Batman), schwa first (a·ttack), schwa second (sa·lad) [Stat25].
- **Spelling voice:** say the weak vowel as the sound it is spelled with ("sa-lad", "mul-ti-ply", "Sep-tem-ber"). "in our talking voice we say 'multiply', but in our spelling voice we say mul-ti-ply" [TTE-PSW]. It also fixes elided sounds: di·ffe·rent, e·ve·ry, go·vern·ment [HFW25]. It is "NOT meant to change the way pupils talk" (founder, DOSSIER §10.5).
- **Year 2 can do it:** "Seemingly demanding words, such as difficult, should be straightforward to spell, even by Year 2, provided that each syllable is carefully enunciated using a spelling voice" [Stat25].
- **Accent.** Schwas depend on accent; teach to the children's accent [Stat25]. The PSC guidance: teach < er > as schwa (farmer, baker, faster, after) in the polysyllabic lessons [PSC24g].
- There is **no schwa unit**. Older documents list a "Unit 50 schwa"; the current scope and sequence ends at 49 and teaches schwa in the polysyllabic strand [DOSSIER §10.5].

### 3.5 Prefixes, suffixes and "special endings"

- **Objective:** "the spelling of some common syllables, such as prefixes and suffixes" [S&S-PSW]. The last record-sheet stage is "common suffixes" [DOSSIER §10.4.4]. No public Sounds~Write document gives a suffix or prefix order (open question).
- **Syllables and sounds first, meaning second.** Morphology "can run in parallel to phonics but it doesn't precede phonics teaching", and should not be taught "in tandem" in the early stages; making plural < s > explicit in Key Stage 1 is fine (founder, DOSSIER §10.8). For *orthodontist*: syllables first, then "the structure and the meaning of the word in morphological terms".
- **-ed** is taught "as inflectional suffixes indicating the simple past tense" [Stat25]; its sounds sit in EC28 (/d/, played) and EC47 (/t/, stopped).
- **Special endings** have no unit numbers: -tion, -sion, -ssion, -cial ("shul"), -cient ("shunt"), -ture (/ch/ + schwa), -ssure ("shuh") [Stat25; Doc38]. Section 4.
- **Rules talk is avoided.** Sounds~Write's accreditation audit asks schools to stop "talking about spelling rules" [DOSSIER §11.1]. The National Curriculum's rules column is non-statutory (section 7.3), so a Sounds~Write school meets the statutory patterns without teaching rules.
- **What the official checks use by the end of Year 2:** prefixes and suffixes as ordinary syllables, e.g. un·hook, un·known, dis·in·fect, im·prove·ment, use·ful, pay·ment, pow·er·ful, slow·ly, ha·ppi·ly, ill·ness, grate·ful, sa·ving, wa·tching [Checks26].

## 4. "Shun" and the other special endings

### 4.1 How Sounds~Write codes it

- "Shun" is **three sounds**: /sh/, schwa, /n/. The 2025 statutory analysis labels *position* "EC special ending - tion representing the three sounds in 'shun'", and *question* the same ending as 'chun' [Stat25].
- The **lexicon** lists < ti >, < ci >, < si > and < ssi > as spellings of /sh/ (action, addition, special, social, tension, extension, permission, discussion) and < si > as a spelling of /zh/ (decision, revision, invasion). The < o > in -tion and the < a > in -cian are schwa spellings like the < o > in *lemon* [Lexicon].
- "The two-letter spelling < ti > only represents the sound 'sh' in three English suffixes" (-tion, -tious, -tial) [Lexicon]. The founder says the same: < ti > is /sh/ "only as part of suffixes, such as 'station', or 'essential'" (DOSSIER §9.13).
- **No Extended Code unit** teaches /sh/ or /zh/ spellings. They are met through the polysyllabic lessons ("common suffixes"), Lesson 10 (< ch > as /ch/, /sh/, /k/: lunch, chef, school), tangential teaching and Lesson 15 [DOSSIER §9.13; PSC24g]. The official consonant word-reading resource has a /zh/ page with *decision*, *division*, *television* [ConsonantWR].
- **At sound level** (Lesson 11) the ending is three tiles, < ti > < o > < n > ("ONE sound per letter tile"); **at syllable level** (Lessons 13–14) 'tion' is handled as one syllable. (Ours: this follows from the tile rule and the lexicon's coding; the members' building boards for suffix words are not public.)
- **First meeting:** since these spellings exist only in long words, they cannot be introduced in one-syllable words. The teacher takes responsibility at first ("This is /sh/. Say /sh/ here.") and teaches the ending formally later with Lessons 11–15. (Ours: the untaught-spelling script [DOSSIER §11.2] applied to Sounds~Write's rule for Units 18/34/35.)

### 4.3 Choosing the spelling

- **Frequency:** -tion is the most common [NC-A1].
- **The root word is the best clue** (National Curriculum, non-statutory guidance): -tion after roots ending t/te (invent → invention), -ssion after ss or -mit (express, permit), -sion after d or se (expand, tense), -cian after c or cs (magic, music, optics, electric) [NC-A1].
- **Sounds~Write's way to the same knowledge** (ours, from its rules): after the word is read and written by syllables, Lesson 15 isolates the hard spelling (< ci > in 'cian') and links it to easy words with the same spelling and sound (magician, musician, optician); then the teacher talks about the root (*magic*, *music*) as meaning, not as a rule. This keeps "syllables first, meaning second" (3.5) and avoids rules talk while giving children the same clue the National Curriculum names.

### 4.5 A worked run for *magician* (ours: official lesson steps, our word)

A sketch for game designers, using only the official lines and correction scripts. Children would first have done Lessons 11–12 with easier words and met < g > as /j/.

**Lesson 11 (build, sound level).** Tiles, jumbled, one sound each: m, a, g, i, ci, a, n. Three lines.
- "When I say 'magician', you can hear three syllables."
- "What's the first syllable you hear when I say 'magician'?" 'ma' (spelling voice) → the child builds m, a, saying /m/ /a/ → "Now let's say the sounds and read the syllable."
- Second syllable 'gi'. If the child reaches for a < j >: "That's a spelling of /j/, but in this word we need this spelling of /j/" (point to < g >).
- Third syllable 'cian'. First time: "This is /sh/. It's two letters, but it's one sound. Say /sh/ here."
- "Now let's say the sounds and read each syllable. Now say the syllables and read the word."
- Writing: "Let's say the word very precisely in its syllables." Write each syllable saying the sounds, then "write the word without the gap between the syllables."

**Lesson 12 (read) on another day, different words from the same family:** mu|si|cian, op|ti|cian with the lines put in.

**Lesson 15 (analyse), a week or more later, once it reads easily:** "Are there any sounds in 'cian' that you might find difficult to spell?" → underline < ci > → easy words with the same spelling of /sh/: musician, optician. If a child offers *station*: "that's the same sound, but it is a different spelling." Then talk about meaning: a magician does magic.

**Dictation:** "The word is magician. 'The magician pulled a rabbit out of a hat.' Write the word magician, say the sounds as you do."

## 5. One spelling, several sounds (and one spelling for two sounds)

- **Concept 4, "Many spellings can represent more than one sound",** is taught formally from Unit 3 with Lesson 10 and the eleven spelling units: < ea > (team, great, bread), < o > (hot, no, do), < ow > (cow, snow), < oo > (moon, book), < ou > (loud, double, soup), < s > (cats, his), < ew > (blew, new), < a > (cat, was, apron, father), < y > (yes, myth, my, happy), < g > (gum, gem), < gh > (laugh, ghost) [S&S-EC].
- **Lesson 10** [DOSSIER §2.3.5]: columns headed by the sound; "In some words, this spelling can be a different sound"; sort by asking "Does this word have the /o/ sound or the /oe/ sound in it?"; follow-up: read it wrongly, "Does that make sense?" Don't let children suggest their own words, and vet words for accent.
- **Reading correction:** "This can be /a/, but in this word, it's /ae/. Say /ae/ here. Say the sounds and read the word" [Split24]. Freshford tells parents "Does that make sense? Try it a different way" [Freshford-Y1Y2].
- **Beyond the spelling units,** tangential pairs appear in Year 1 for the PSC: < ch > as /ch/, /sh/, /k/ (lunch, chef, school); < g > as /j/ (gem); < s > as /z/ (hens); < c > as /s/ (cell) [PSC24g]. In the PSC, pseudo-words accept either reading: 'meast' with /ee/ or /ae/, 'strow' with /ow/ or /oe/ [PSC24g].
- **One spelling for two sounds:** < x > (/k/ /s/, box), < u > and < ue > for /y/ /oo/ (unit, statue: "one spelling, but two sounds"), < eue > in queue. Word lists keep < u > as one spelling "to prevent students spelling it as < y > < oo >" [Stat25; PSC25a]. The lexicon treats /eer/ as /ee/ + schwa and /ue/ as "a little 'fiction'" kept for children [Lexicon].
- The game's Sound Detective design (`docs/MULTI_SOUND.md`) covers concept 4 in detail.

## 6. High-frequency ("tricky") words

- **Sounds~Write has no "tricky words".** It calls them words "that contain code which has not yet been taught"; high-frequency words are "not … synonymous with 'sight words'"; it quotes the DfE Reading Framework: "Children should not be asked to learn lists of high frequency words" [HFW25; HB26].
- **The teacher takes responsibility.** Reading: point to the untaught spelling and give its sound ("This is /e/, say /e/ here, /s/ /e/ /d/, 'said'"); writing: model the word and have the child copy it saying the sounds [HB26; DOSSIER §11.2]. Practise a recurring word by word building with the hard spelling on one tile; no more than one "difficult" bit per word and one or two such words a week (founder, DOSSIER §11.3).

## 7. What a child should read and spell by the end of Year 1 and Year 2

### 7.1 National Curriculum, Year 1

**Statutory word reading** [NC-PoS]: apply phonic knowledge as "the route to decode words"; respond speedily with the right sound for graphemes "for all 40+ phonemes, including, where applicable, alternative sounds for graphemes"; read unfamiliar words with taught GPCs; read common exception words; read words with -s, -es, -ing, -ed, -er and -est; "read other words of more than one syllable that contain taught GPCs"; read contractions; read decodable books aloud and re-read them.

**Statutory spelling** [NC-PoS]: spell words with each of the 40+ phonemes taught; common exception words; the days of the week; name the letters of the alphabet (and use letter names "to distinguish between alternative spellings of the same sound"); add -s/-es, un-, and -ing, -ed, -er, -est where the root doesn't change; "write from memory simple sentences dictated by the teacher".

**Appendix 1, Year 1** [NC-A1] (the left column is statutory; guidance and example words are not): ff, ll, ss, zz, ck; /ng/ spelt n before k (bank, think); **division of words into syllables** (pocket, rabbit, carrot, thunder, sunset; "Words of more than one syllable often have an unstressed syllable in which the vowel sound is unclear"); -tch; v at the end spelt ve; -s and -es; -ing, -ed, -er; -er and -est; the vowel digraphs and trigraphs (ai oi ay oy a–e e–e i–e o–e u–e ar ee ea er ir ur oo oa oe ou ow ue ew ie igh or ore aw au air ear are); -y as /ee/; ph and wh; k before e, i, y; the prefix un-; compound words; common exception words (the, a, do, to, today, of, said, says, are, were, was, is, his, has, I, you, your, they, be, he, me, she, we, no, go, so, by, my, here, there, where, love, come, some, one, once, ask, friend, school, put, push, pull, full, house, our – "and/or others, according to the programme used").

### 7.3 National Curriculum, Year 2

**Statutory word reading** [NC-PoS]: decode "until automatic decoding has become embedded and reading is fluent"; read words with "alternative sounds for graphemes"; "read accurately words of two or more syllables that contain the same graphemes as above"; read words with common suffixes; further common exception words; "read most words quickly and accurately, without overt sounding and blending"; read closely matched books. | Spelling | new spellings reach writing 5–7 units after teaching, so first spellings of about EC1–20 | about EC1–43 accurate in writing; first + more spellings consolidated in Y3 |
| High-frequency words | code for 233 of the 300 Letters and Sounds words | all of them |
| Long words | two- and three-syllable words, Lessons 11–12, code to about EC20 (check 18: level, report, nightmare) | syllable-level Lessons 13–14 and Lesson 15; code to about EC45 (check 38: mechanic, character, chemistry); common suffixes; spelling voice for schwa |
| Assessment | PSC (threshold 32/40); progress checks every 6–8 weeks | progress checks; PSC retake for those who need it |

Sources: [HB26; HFW25; PlanY1; PlanY2; Checks26]. The spelling rows are our arithmetic from the official lags.

### 7.5 Where the National Curriculum and Sounds~Write timings differ

| Item | NC year | Sounds~Write | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| < ore >, < ear > (dear), < ie > (chief) | Y1 | EC43, EC49, EC29 (Y2); < ie > optional in EC2 | Y1 marketing shouldn't claim these unless taught tangentially |
| /zh/ spelt s (television, treasure) | Y2 | no unit; tangential, lexicon | the game needs its own /zh/ content |
| -tion | Y2 | "special ending" in the long-word strand; not in the checks | teach by late Y2; label as special endings |
| -ment, -ness, -ful, -less, -ly | Y2 | common syllables in long words (payment, useful, slowly in the checks) | fine |
| -cian, -sion, -ssion, -ture, -sure | Y3–4 | suffix card set; long-word strand | stretch content for late Y2 |
| rules (doubling, y → i, e-dropping) | Y2 (non-statutory guidance) | not taught as rules | patterns only, through long-word lessons |

## 8. Skills and typical errors

| Skill | What it looks like in Y1–Y2 | Typical errors | Sounds~Write response (short) | Signal the game can record |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Segmenting** | spells words sound by sound, including clusters and syllables | leaves out a sound in a cluster (*spint*); adds a sound; wrong order | stretch and point to where the sound belongs; "If this were 'bat', this would be /b/. Is this /b/?" | slot-level errors with position |
| **Blending** | reads new words, then syllables, then long words, less overtly by Y2 | guesses from the first letters; reads through without stopping at syllables | "Say the sounds and read the word"; cover and blend; put the syllable lines back | first-try accuracy, latency, guesses |
| **Phoneme manipulation** | Sound Swap with Initial Code and nonsense words; trying a different sound for a spelling | swaps the wrong position | gesture and stretch the changing sound; never segment for the child | swap step type and position |
| **Code knowledge** | knows several spellings per sound and several sounds per spelling | reads a spelling with its other sound (*bead* as 'bed'); letter names; says a two-letter spelling as two sounds | "It can be /e/, but in this word it's /ee/"; "It's two letters but it's one sound" | per spelling → sound (GPC) proficiency, read vs spell |
| **Syllabification** | hears and marks syllables; later splits words itself | builds a different split from the one said; **cuts one spelling in two** (bot-tle as b-o-t-t); **drops a syllable** (*multication*); adds one | "You said 'fan — tas — tic' but you've built fant — as — tic"; say it precisely in its syllables | syllable count and split per word |
| **Stress and schwa** | finds the strong syllable; spells weak syllables with a spelling voice | *Septemba*, *chickin*, *diffrent*, *evry*, *devision* | "in our talking voice we say 'multiply', but in our spelling voice we say mul-ti-ply" | schwa-syllable accuracy |
| **Spelling choice** | picks the right spelling of a sound for this word; knows the common ones first | a real but wrong spelling (*rayn*, *steem*, *werk*, *Septembur*, *fantastick*, *magition*): the commonest Extended Code error, expected for 5–7 units | "This is a way of spelling /ae/, but in this word we need this spelling"; "What's the difficult bit for you?" | which alternative was chosen, per word |
| **Suffixes and endings** | adds common endings as syllables; special endings by late Y2 | ending spelled by sound (*jumpt*, *stashun*); *hopeing*, *happyness*, *babys* | treat the ending as a syllable with its own spelling; model it | ending-level accuracy |
| **Proofreading** | reads back every word and sentence written | doesn't notice a missing sound or wrong spelling | read back after writing "to confirm that what they wanted was what they got"; dictation read back 2–3 times | self-correction rate |
| **Fluency** | Year 2: reads most words without sounding out | slow, laboured reading of known words | SpeedRead: "20 words read correctly in 30 seconds = achieved" | words per 30 seconds |

Sources: [TTE-PSW; Split24; DOSSIER §4, §11, §13; PSC24a; PSC25a; NC-PoS]. The official Progress Tracker records reading errors as "No attempt", "Unable to blend", "Guess", and per spelling "Not said", "Wrong sound", "Letter name", "Said separately", "+ Sound added" [DOSSIER §13.6]. What Sounds~Write never does: supply the whole word for a single untaught spelling, encourage guessing from pictures or context, say letters "make" sounds, talk about "silent letters" or "magic e", or segment the word for a child who is spelling [DOSSIER §4.10].

## 9. What this means for Super Ninja

### 9.1 The middle and end game

- **The escalation axes for Years 1–2** are: more spellings per sound (4 → 12 for /or/); spellings with several sounds (the spelling units); longer words (2 → 5 syllables); weak syllables and schwa; common endings (-ing, -ed, -er, -est, un-, -ment, -ness, -ful, -less, -ly, then -tion and the other special endings). A Year 2 land should feel harder because its words are longer and its spellings less common, not only because the sound on the flag is new.
- **Long words as a parallel path.** From EC4 week 2, each land carries long-word levels built from units 4–7 behind (the official lag), moving from Lessons 11–12 (teacher splits, sound level) to 13–14 (child splits, syllable level) and 15 (analysis). This gives every Extended Code spelling a second life, which is what Sounds~Write intends ("constant recycling").
- **Land size.** Sounds~Write's 6–8-week check rhythm suggests about three to four sound units per land: roughly six lands in Year 1 (checks 2–18) and six in Year 2 (checks 19–38), close to the arc in `SOUNDS_WRITE_MODEL.md` §6.1.
- **Late Year 2 set piece: special endings.** The "shun" family (station, magician, optician, musician, division, television) fits a finale for Year 2 and a bridge into Year 3, labelled as a stretch.

### 9.2 Bosses

- **The panda is the boss of Units 1–2.** Sounds~Write has no check before Unit 3 ("we don't provide a progress check before Unit 3"), so the first boss is game-only; that is fine as long as later bosses follow the checks.
- **A check-shaped boss** (official rules [Checks26; ChecksFAQ]): choose it by the last unit completed; word reading with at least a one-unit lag, spelling with at least two; up to 20 words, 3–12 of them long words from check 3 onward; 2–4 dictated long words from check 4; one dictated sentence; a word read only after a correction counts as an error for the record, and a spelling counts only if the whole word is right.
- **So bosses scale by themselves:** the end-of-Year-1 boss reads *level*, *report*, *nightmare* and dictates Unit 19 long words; the end-of-Year-2 boss reads *mechanic*, *character*, *chemistry*. A boss can mirror the monster's world while testing the land's units, which answers "is the panda the boss of lame sounds?": yes, and each later boss is the boss of that land's longer words.

### 9.3 Marketing: claims we can back

| Claim | True when | Proof to show |
|---|---|---|
| "Built on Sounds~Write, the programme at school" | now (official order, language and lessons) | the teacher lines on screen; the unit label |
| "Your Year 1 child reads and spells two-syllable words the way school teaches: syllable by syllable" | once PW1–PW3 levels with sound-level segs are playable | a Lesson 11 build of *sunset* or *dentist* with the syllable lines |
| "Ready for the Phonics Screening Check" | only when EC1–25 content, pseudo-words and four two-syllable words per check are playable (today the game reaches about EC11) | an adapted-PSC-style boss |
| "By the end of Year 2: *multiply*, *magazine*, *mechanic*, *chemistry*" | when EC27–49 and the long-word review are playable | the official check words, built and read |
| "*magician*, *optician*, *division*, *multiplication*" | only with special-ending content; frame as "Year 2 stretch, Year 3 at school" | a Lesson 11 run of *magician* (4.5) and a Lesson 15 analysis |
| "All 44 sounds / the complete code" | only when EC49 content is playable | the World Flower complete |

Avoid: "tricky words" and "sight words", "letters make sounds", "magic e", "silent letters", spelling-rule claims, and any use of Sounds~Write's logo or materials.

### 9.4 Content problems found (for the content workflows; read-only here)

1. **Long words have no sounds.** `src/content/units/PW*.ts` and the `poly` arrays in `EC*.ts` give `syllables` and `schwa` only; `PolyItem` in `src/content/sw.ts` expects `segs` per syllable. Without them Lesson 11 (one tile per sound) can't be generated.
2. **Syllable splits differ from Sounds~Write's printed splits** (official form in brackets [Checks26]): hol|i|day (ho|li|day), an|i|mal (a|ni|mal), el|e|phant (e|le|phant), ep|i|sode (e|pi|sode), mag|a|zine (ma|ga|zine), heav|i|ly (hea|vi|ly), hes|i|tate (he|si|tate), to|geth|er (to|ge|ther), me|chan|ic (me|cha|nic), chem|is|try (che|mis|try), vol|can|ic (vol|ca|nic), au|to|mat|ic (au|to|ma|tic), dis|in|fect (di|sin|fect), dan|ger|ous (dan|ge|rous), con|tain|er (con|tai|ner), oys|ter (oy|ster), anch|or (an|chor), po|wer|ful (pow|er|ful).
3. **Five splits cut one spelling in two:** bot|tle, rot|ten, hap|pi|ly, but|ter|fly, to|mor|row (Sounds~Write: bo|ttle, ro|tten, ha|ppi|ly, bu|tter|fly, to|mo|rrow). This breaks "one sound per tile".
4. **Schwa flags:** *musician* marks syllable 0 as schwa ('mu' is /m ue/); *location* has no flag on 'tion'; *multiplication* flags only 'tion' although 'ti' and 'pli' are weak.
5. **EC18 lacks its own spellings.** `EC18.ts` has only < l >, < ll >, < le > one-syllable words; none with < al el il ol > (pencil, medal, camel, pupil, petrol, pedal), which Sounds~Write teaches in two-syllable words from day one; its `poly` pool is Initial Code words.
6. **Almost no special-ending content:** location, musician, multiplication (PW5/6/8/9). No magician, optician, division, station, television, picture.
7. **World Flower gaps** (`src/content/flower.ts`): the /sh/ petal lacks < si > (tension, extension; NC Y3–4); the /ch/ petal lacks < t > (picture, nature: -ture); the schwa petal has a e o u er our but not ure, ar, or, ai, ou (picture, collar, doctor, curtain, famous). The /zh/ petal is fine (s, si, ge).

### 9.5 What the learner model should record for long words

Per word: syllable count and split chosen; per syllable: correct first try, schwa handled; per spelling: GPC key (including ti>sh, ci>sh, si>zh, ssi>sh, t>ch, ure>schwa); lesson level (11–15) and help given. Scoring stays as in `SOUNDS_WRITE_MODEL.md` §3 (independent first tries only). Long-word review should target units 4–7 behind the current unit.
