# The playtest treadmill

The aim is for machines to produce feedback so humans don't have to. One command sends bots and AI playtesters through the whole game and turns everything they notice into one de-duplicated inbox: `playtest/INBOX.md`. Fix what's in it, rerun, and the fixed items close themselves.

```
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts --quick         # ~1–2 min: one level of each kind, bots + Jev
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts                 # ~10–15 min: every level + monkey + visual critic + Jev
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts --personas      # + Codex persona playtesters (30–60 min, run it while you sleep)
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts --watch         # quick run after every change under src/
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts --joins         # + "one take, or joined?": spliced speech judged by ear (~3 min)
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts --soak          # + the soak check: 8 levels in one page, judged against the perf budgets (~12 min)
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts --soak-phone    # + the same on a slow phone (CPU 4× slower), 12 levels (~25 min, nightly)
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts --script        # + the script checks: four continuous runs, then script-audit --check (~40–60 min)
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts --script --watcher   # the same, plus the watcher from the start to w2-1 (the paw at every Ready hold)
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts --sounds        # + the sound display check: sound-display --check, every case, then the splitter run (~20 min)
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts --no-petals     # the sweep without its petal and perf invariants (they are the default since 27 Sep)
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts --script --sounds --no-bots   # just those two, without the sweep and the critic
bun scripts/treadmill/run.ts --help          # every flag; an unknown flag prints this and runs nothing
bun scripts/treadmill/<any script>.ts --help # every treadmill script prints its usage and exits (scripts/lib/help.ts)
```

By default a run plays a frozen build of the working tree (`frozen.ts`, served on :4180), so edits made mid-run can't reload its pages. `--live` and `--watch` play the dev server on :5173 instead (`bun run dev`).

### Talk anywhere, and questions that don't fade

Verify round 2 found three things the rows above passed. `talk-before-action` counts a level's own talk at a game's first meeting, so the talk after a level (its close running into the reward: 17–27 s in w1-4, w1-6 and w1-7) and the rewards themselves (Reward 2: 29.7 s from "Starfish!" to "Tap your petal…") were never measured. And `line-60s` passed w1-12's twelve swaps, because the question rotated through three recorded variants, each at most twice in 60 s. A run of talk is TEACHER_SCRIPT §6's: from a tap the game registers (not a tile tapped while the scene is busy, not Home or Back) to where the line that cues the next action starts (the last line before the next tap, or before a question or a held step opens). Not after a tap on the paw (the demo the child asked to see again). The limit is the age band's (ARCHITECTURE §6.4): 12 s for a brand-new child who isn't at school yet, 15 s at Reception and for every `--from` run, 18 s from Year 1.

| Metric | Target | What it counts |
|---|---|---|
| `talk-reward` | the age band's | Every run of talk that is said in a reward, a World Flower trip or on the map, or runs into one from the level before it (a clip's own route, recorded by `continuous.ts`). n/a in a journey, which has no rewards between its pages. |
| `talk-longest` | the age band's | The longest run of talk anywhere in the session: levels (every play, not only first meetings), rewards, trips, the map, the welcome. The value names where the runs over the limit are ("level→reward 10, level 5"). Films and Story Time's book text are watched, so their runs don't count. |
| `ask-cycle` | 0 | One whole question (a line ending in "?"), in any of its rotated variants, on more than 3 items running in a game with no miss between. A line's variants are the lines that ask the same idea in `line-tags.ts` (`as: "ask"`; "What do we need to change?", "Which sound changes?" and "Which sound needs to change?" are one). SCRIPT_STYLE §5: a stem at most three times running, and a whole question fades after two first-try answers in a row (SCRIPT_FIXES A6; TEACHER_SCRIPT §3.20), until a miss. An item is the child's answer, so an idle re-ask with no tap between is the same item. A miss (a wrong sound, a correction line, a lost streak, a split) starts the count again. Not counted: lead-in stems that carry the item ("Which one starts with…" /m/, which rotate by design, SCRIPT_FIXES A5), the Ready lines, and the per-item questions TEACHER_SCRIPT says every time (Kai and Suki's "Who read it right?", W2's rows, the corrections and idle re-asks). |

### The fast and slow checks

Jonas, 27 Sep: "explain more often that there are slow and fast ways to read words". TEACHER_SCRIPT §9 is the script and FIX_PLAN §13.5 the targets. `script-audit.ts --check` reads them from the transcripts. A session is one page: a continuous run is one session, and in a journey each level is its own session. A land is the level's world.

| Metric | Target | What it counts |
|---|---|---|
| `fs-per-session` | 0 missing | Every game with a fast/slow moment (§9.3: fastslow, slowpick, tapall:in, sounds, dots, firstsound, build, readcheck, soundhunt, battle, boss, review, trial, swap, run, story) says an idea line or a rabbit prompt at least once a session. Ninja Run may use `tv_fs_run` or `tv_run_lanterns`, and Kai and Suki, Ninja Run and Story Time may use the no-tap pair's `tv_fs_now_fast`. In lands 1–2 every such game needs one; from land 3, only the session's first game with a read-back. |
| `fs-repeat` | 0 | An idea line (the `tv_fs_` block of lines.ts, less the lead-ins, `tv_fs_run`, the stuck lines and the praise lines: 19 on 27 Sep), a fast/slow praise line or `tv_same_word` said twice in a session. A line cut off the first time may come again. `t_if_you_say_sounds` is exempt, because W5's close says it every time. |
| `fs-idea-caps` | ≤ 2 / ≤ 4; 1 game from land 3 | Idea lines per level and per session (with `t_if_you_say_sounds` and `tv_same_word`). From land 3, Move 1 or an idea line in more than one game a session also fails. |
| `fs-readback` | 100 % | The first read-back of each game type in a session (after its Ready, on a full form) must go slow, then fast. A read-back is the word's own sounds (or its slow word), then the word, with nothing between but Sensei's lines and the rabbit (a tap that said nothing, as a learner's second tap on a dot, is no answer). A read-back in the paw's replay of a demo (Show me again at a Ready, until the next Ready) is Sensei's, not the child's first, so neither this row nor `fs-badges` counts it. The slot needs a slow lead-in just before it (`tv_fs_say_sounds_slow`, `tv_fs_say_slow`, `tv_fs_slow_tortoise`), and the word needs a fast lead before it (`tv_fs_rabbit_read`, `fm_tap_rabbit`, `tv_fs_now_fast`, `tv_fs_fast_rabbit`). From land 3, only the session's first read-back is judged, and the gem battle has none. |
| `fs-badges` | 100 % | In those games, the tortoise lit on every slow slot (a slow word, a read-back's sounds) and the rabbit lit on every read-back's fast word. The evidence is a nav log `speed` entry for it from 1.5 s before to 0.8 s after the start, or its being the last one lit (within 6 s), or the page's own lighting as a clip of the slot started (`lit`). |
| `fs-answer-leak` | 0 | In Slow Words, Guess My Word, Ninja Run and Kai and Suki, the answer said before the child's first answer to the question: the answer's word (the turn's `next`, or the lantern the bot flew at), or `tv_idle_look_<w>` at all. | `fs-rabbit` | all; 0 outside | Every rabbit prompt is answered by a nav log `rabbit` entry (tap or timeout) or a tap on the warm-up's own rabbit before the word. A rabbit tap with no prompt in the 25 s before it means the rabbit was live when it shouldn't be. |
| `fs-talk` | ≤ 12 s | Talk before a child action in the runs that include a fast/slow line. The rabbit's tap is an action, so it splits the talk. `continuous.ts` records what these read. The bot never waits on a tap for Playwright's 30 s default (every tap and look has a short timeout): one such wait, as Ninja Eyes' tiles left for Word Building, held the bot for 2 game minutes while the game's idle ladder spoke, and made two false `fs-talk` failures of 38 s (verify round 1). It turns the nav log's `speed` and `rabbit` entries into events (a rabbit tap that the tap log missed becomes a tap). On every speech clip it records `lit`: which of the tortoise and the rabbit were lit as the clip started, sampled then and again 150 ms in. The transcript shows `[tortoise]` and `[rabbit]` among the sounds, and "(the rabbit: …)" lines. Clip lengths come from `durations.ts`: durations.json, except that the gapped slow words (x/) and any clip newer than durations.json are measured from the file (ffprobe's length, without spawning ffprobe). The bot taps a live nav rabbit (`__snNav.speed.rabbit === "live"`, `[data-fs="rabbit"].live`) once it has finished growing (at most 1.5 s). A warm-up's own rabbit is the scene's answer (`__snState.next`).

**What the checks read from the game** (FS-F3.1's nav layer, src/ui/nav.tsx; the scenes' own badges in the warm-ups):
- the nav log: `{ kind: "speed", which: "slow" | "fast" | null, via, shown }` each time the tortoise or the rabbit lights (or goes out), including the tortoise a `stretch:` clip lights by itself; an entry with `shown: false` (the nav badges not drawn) lights nothing the child sees. `{ kind: "rabbit", how: "tap" | "timeout" | "slow" }` as Move 1's wait ends (or its tortoise is tapped);
- the badges: `.fs-badge[data-fs="tortoise"]` and `.fs-badge[data-fs="rabbit"]`, lit with class `lit`, the waiting rabbit with class `live` (aria-label "The rabbit", which the tap log records), and `__snNav.speed = { shown, lit, rabbit: "live" | null }`. The probes also accept `[data-speed="slow" | "fast"]` and `data-lit`/`data-live`;
- the warm-ups' own buttons: `.wu-speed.tortoise` and `.wu-speed.rabbit`, lit with `glow`, `flash` or `pulse`.

Every source writes the same `Finding` shape (`types.ts`): `sig`, `source`, `severity`, `case`, `title`, `detail`, `evidence[]` and `repro`. Adding a new checker means writing `<runDir>/<name>.json` in that shape and adding it to the list in `inbox.ts`.

## Frozen builds

Test on a frozen build, never on the shared dev server, which reloads the page whenever anyone saves:

```
bun scripts/treadmill/frozen.ts --port 4404                    # build into playtest/runs/frozen/.build-4404, serve it, print the URL
bun scripts/treadmill/frozen.ts --port 4404 --probes --detach  # with soak.ts's module probes; leave it running in the background
bun scripts/treadmill/frozen.ts --port 4404 --stop             # stop a detached one
```

`--out <dir>` picks the folder. `--no-build` serves what is already there. `--retry N` waits 45 s and rebuilds when another agent's half-finished edit breaks the build. `--probes` builds with `soak.vite.config.ts`, which is the game plus read-only probes into module state (live particles, the ninja's effect nodes, listener sets, the decoded-audio cache, the save's `adjustLog`), exposed as `window.__snPerfMods`. That's for test builds only. `soak.ts` and `run.ts` use the same code (`import { frozen } from "./frozen"`).

## Stills

`sound-shots.ts <out-dir> <name> <url> [ms,ms,...] [save-json] --base URL [--freeze <ms>]` takes 844×390 stills at set times. `--freeze <ms>` pauses every CSS, Web and SVG (SMIL) animation at that time before each shot, so a before build and an after build can be compared frame for frame. Like `sound-display.ts`, it plays `--base URL`, `--build dir` (served on `--port`) or a fresh frozen build, never the dev server unless named.

## The soak check

```
bun scripts/treadmill/soak.ts --mobile --cpu 4 --fast 2 --levels 12 --idle 60 --stress 150 --check   # the phone, ~25 min
bun scripts/treadmill/soak.ts --fast 2 --levels 20 --check                                             # desktop, ~30 min
bun scripts/treadmill/soak.ts --levels 4 --idle 20 --stress 60 --check                                 # a smoke, ~7 min
bun scripts/treadmill/soak.ts --serve none --base http://127.0.0.1:4404 ...   # play a frozen build that is already up (built with --probes)
bun scripts/treadmill/soak.ts --analyse <run dir> --check                     # re-judge a finished run, e.g. after a budget changes
```

The after-each-level sample waits until Sensei has been quiet for 2.5 s (at most 25 s), then starts the map's idle ladder again with a touch on nothing just before its 2 s window, so the ninja's hop (8 s) and the stone's hint (16 s of game time) don't land in it. A sample in which anything was said anyway (a speech clip started from then to its end, which the soak counts with a `window.__audioLog` that only counts, or a `say()` still running) is taken again, up to twice; `speechDuring` in each sample says how many clips it heard. (A hint said during the memory dump that ended inside the window read "not speaking" at the window's end, while the lipsync asked for 60 frames a second.) ## The transcript personas

`continuous.ts --persona` (and `bot.ts`, which reads `window.__botPersona`): **perfect**; **learner** (about 1 in 3 first tries wrong); **splitter**, a learner's pace that splits two-letter spellings on purpose: on the first try of any slot whose spelling has two or more letters it taps a tile that is part of it (< s > for < sh >, < t > or < ch > for < tch >: the game's `splitsSpelling`), wherever that tile is on screen, then listens to the whole correction. It waits (answering nothing) while the slot's letters aren't on screen yet, so a held explanation or a demo can't use up its chance, and it only counts a slot once it has split it. How many splits it can make is up to the game's letter banks: a part of a two-letter spelling is on screen only when the bank happens to offer one (< s > is a regular distractor for < sh >; < c > and < k > never are for < ck >, because they spell the same sound). On 27 Sep, `--from w5-1 --levels 6` made 5, 2, 4, 6 and 6 splits in five runs; the run's last line counts the two-letter slots that offered no part (`noSplit` in its JSON, with each bank); **watcher**, which taps the paw once at every Ready hold. Every persona answers one hand-over Ready in three with the right answer (TEACHER_SCRIPT T4), and waits 300 ms at each new Ready hold before tapping ▶, as a child hears the question start (an instant tap cut the Ready line before it began). In the World Flower the bots tap a petal join-in (`[data-join="petal"]`, "Tap it, and hear its sound.") once Sensei has asked, while its step still plays: until verify round 1 the held step's wait came first in `step()`, and no bot ever tapped one.

## The accent judge

Is a Sensei line really Southern British? `scripts/accent-judge.ts` answers that for one word at a time, on three markers: the **BATH vowel** (the /ɑː/ of "father" in fast, last, after, ask, not the /æ/ of "cat"), a **rhotic r** (no r in word, car, first) and a **flapped t** (a real t in water and petal, not a soft "d"). It gives each target word a British probability (its share of British votes) and each clip the lowest of its words.

```
doppler run -p os-legacy-2026-04 -c dev -- bun scripts/accent-judge.ts tv_fs_made tv_ts_fast       # line ids (public/a/l)
doppler run -p os-legacy-2026-04 -c dev -- bun scripts/accent-judge.ts clip.mp3 --text "Now say it fast."
doppler run -p os-legacy-2026-04 -c dev -- bun scripts/accent-judge.ts --calibrate-only
```

The target words are found in the text (`--bath`, `--r` and `--flap` name them instead), with 21 votes a word by default (`--votes`). The judge is Gemini `gemini-3.1-pro-preview` with the fast-and-slow checker's prompt (the sentence, then "which vowel did THIS speaker actually use in 'fast'", with "between" allowed), on the clip with 400 ms of silence in front. The r and the flap are asked ABX, against a British and an American "Bird." (or "Water."), then "is the r in 'word' like clip 1's or clip 2's?": asked directly, it heard half of the American reference's flaps as British, and was unsure of a British "Car." (`refs.json` `anchors` says why).

**It calibrates on every run and refuses to judge when that fails** (exit 2). Before judging, it judges the reference clips in `playtest/voice-picker/audio/accent-refs/` (ElevenLabs Alice and George, British, against OpenAI coral with no accent instruction, American, reading the same sentences) on each marker it is about to judge. A marker passes only when the British references get at least 85 % British votes, the American ones at most 15 % (25 % for the r: coral's own r is not always r-coloured), and 90 % of the reference words land on the right side of 50 %. On 27 Sep: BATH 91–100 % against 0–6 %, r 91–100 % against 7–18 %, the flap 100 % against 0 %. On voices it was not calibrated on (macOS Daniel and Samantha, and Sensei's clips that the accent audit had measured), it agreed with the acoustics except on a word-final r said alone ("Car.", "Four."), where it is unsure. So trust it most for an r before a consonant ("word", "World").

**Gate a take at 80 % British on every target word, with 21 votes.** Screening with 6 votes and confirming with 15 more is cheaper. The judge the audio lanes used before (3 votes, "British /ɑː/ or American /æ/?", no calibration) passed takes that this one hears as in between. **A take picked as the best of many needs 21 fresh votes (42 in all)**: the best of 20 takes on 21 votes is often lucky (27 Sep: two picked at 81 % and 86 % came out at 69 % and 55 % on 42 more).

**The words around a word change its accent.** Erinome's "word" was r-coloured in all 20 takes of "Every word is made of sounds, one by one." and British in most takes of "…, one after another." (the F3 measure of `scripts/voice-picker/audit-measure.py` agrees: 0.54–0.75 of her usual F3 against 0.93–0.97). So when a line stays American after many takes, try a few wordings as raw takes (6 each, 6 votes a word) before more takes of the same one. The retake loop of 27 Sep and its results are in `docs/TEACHER_SCRIPT.md` §9.8.
