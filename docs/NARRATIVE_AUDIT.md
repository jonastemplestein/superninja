# Narrative explanation audit

26 September 2026. British English. Audit only; no game source files changed.

## Scope and how to read the counts

I read the perfect and learner [journey transcripts](../playtest/transcripts/2026-09-26-07-18/) in level order, including their sound, stretched-word and story-page events; the corresponding JSON was used to count exact recorded lines and timings. I checked the line catalogue, the 205 generated teacher sentence clips, `teach.ts`, `stories.ts`, the scene triggers and `App.tsx`, `worlds.ts`, the Sounds~Write model and data, the three research sections, the pedagogy and feedback notes, and [the first-five-minutes specification](FIRST_MINUTES.md). That last file appeared after the 07:18 journey run; it is an implementation plan, not evidence that its new lines have played.

The logs contain **68 separately run cases**: training, placement, then 66 levels. Each case starts from a fresh save and a deep link. **They are ordered evidence, not a single child’s continuous save.** The opening film, hero selection, voluntary World Flower and Word Book visits, and gem trials are absent. A zero means *not heard in either supplied journey*, even if the line exists and can play on an optional path. The generated teacher sentences in `teach-lines.gen.ts` have **zero recorded plays** in these journeys because `teach.ts` is used on the optional Flower screen, not in the core lessons. Sound and word clips are recorded separately and are not counted as an explanatory sentence. Story `kind: story` events are spoken page audio; page IDs are cited as `stories:sN:page`. The transcript generator renders story title audio as `[story sN_title]` because its text map contains pages only; the title words were checked against `stories.ts`, but their audio wording cannot be independently verified from these logs.

The inventory’s **P/L** column gives counted explanatory episodes in the perfect and learner journeys. An instruction, reward announcement or question alone does not count as an explanation; the row’s spacing note reports these exposures separately. “Before?” means before the first required action in the sampled path. “Conditional” marks a film or optional screen that can provide context in a normal route but was not sampled. For future Sounds~Write units, “not yet” means the current six worlds do not yet require the concept. Verdicts use **missing**, **once only**, **too late**, or **fine**; a future “missing” is a content-build obligation rather than a present child-facing failure.

The first eight Bamboo stops in `worlds.ts` are game-only pre-code and early Initial Code work. The source model’s **Decisions** section takes precedence over a stale later recommendation in the same document: future words such as *gate* are `g·a·te`, with `< a >` for /ae/ and `< te >` for /t/. No split-spelling narration is proposed.

## Gaps and order of explanation

1. **The first game asks for a word from separate sounds without saying what the exercise means.** `words_made` is “Words are made of sounds. Listen!” but has no call site and is heard 0/0. `listen_sounds` starts at w1-1 93.3s in the perfect trace. The earlier slow section starts at 50.8s, with no line saying that a fast and stretched pronunciation are the same word. This is Jonas’s Round 13 observation reproduced by the transcript.
2. **The reward system precedes its optional tutorials.** At w1-2, `petals_got` says “You won back some sounds!” (14/14 across the journey). At w1-4, the perfect trace first plays `gem_energy` and `book_new`. The Flower’s `flower_i1`–`flower_i6` and Book’s `book_i1`–`book_i3` play only when their screens are opened and play 0/0 in both supplied traces. The film can explain the flower on the normal opening path; the deep-linked evidence cannot establish that a child heard it. Both `IntroFilm.tsx` and `Intros.tsx` allow taps to skip spoken steps, and `seenIntro` is later set after hero selection, so a “seen” flag does not prove the explanation played. The game should introduce each reward *at the first reward* and track whether the relevant line actually finished.
3. **Two-letter and three-letter spellings are not explained when introduced in the recorded lesson path.** w3-6 shows `< ff >`, `< ll >`, `< ss >`, `< zz >`; w5-1 shows `< sh >`, `< ch >`, `< th >`; w5-6 shows `< tch >`; w6-6 shows `< igh >`. `two_letters_one_sound`, `three_letters_one_sound`, `t_two_letters` and `t_three_letters` are all 0/0. `Dojo.tsx` queues them after `dojo_this_sound`, but the trace shows the bot tapping the newly revealed tile before those clips begin. The only heard two-letter statement is Baron’s w5-11 taunt, long after first need. The teacher-language research says to point out “two letters, one sound” **at every opportunity**.
4. **First and middle position definitions can be cut off; the last position is never defined.** The perfect path hears `first_intro` only at w1-10, after first-sound tasks in w1-2 and w1-3. It hears `hunt_intro` at w1-11, after w1-7. The learner hears more repeats because levels restart. `last_sound_q` is frequent, but it asks without explaining where “last” is. A left-to-right cue is absent before the first sound lines and builds.
5. **The game uses one spelling with different sounds without naming the idea.** w5-1 teaches `< th >` and `th=dh`; `sameSpelling()` exists in `teach.ts` but has no call site. The official model places formal concept 4 at EC3 `< ea >`, which the current Sky Temple skips. `< x >` for /k/+/s/ is a separate exception at w3-6 and also has no core-lesson explanation.
6. **Adjacent consonants receive a vague, late cue.** Unit 8 starts at w4-1. `dojo_longer` (“Some sounds sit very close together”) is heard at w4-3 and w4-6 in the perfect path, after w4-1 and w4-2. It never explicitly says to listen to each adjacent consonant separately.
7. **Formal same-sound sorting arrives early in the teaching order.** `sort_start` is heard before the first w3-2 sort, so its immediate instruction is timely. But `worlds.ts` places c/k sorting in IC5 and l/ll, s/ss sorts in IC7, while `SOUNDS_WRITE_MODEL.md` says concept 3 is met informally in IC11 and formally in the Bridging Unit. This is a progression issue: a clearer line alone cannot correct the sequence.
8. **A normal first story choice has no played choice instruction.** w1-14 speaks `story_your_turn`, but `story_choose` plays 0/0. The child meets the option words at the fork without a spoken instruction to read and choose. The first read-page use of “the” and “is” in w2-7 has no point-of-need special-word teaching.
9. **Future scope is not yet in the six worlds.** Four-letter spellings, consonant+e words from EC1, EC3 alternative sounds for `< ea >`, polysyllabic words and syllables, and the official EC1–49 progression are in `sw.ts`/the model but not the current `worlds.ts` journey. `sw.ts` is not imported by the current levels. These are future explanation requirements, not observed failures of a current item.

## Repetition: proposed spaced schedule

The schedule counts *concept reminders*, not repeated full tutorials. Short exact line options and implementation triggers are in the fix list. For a learner who repeats a level after errors, store concept exposure per save so a level restart does not play the same long introduction three times back to back. Continue to repeat the Sounds~Write core wording in context.

## Language audit

**Sounds~Write forbidden language.** I found no literal child-facing use of letters that “say” or “make” sounds, “magic e”, “silent letters”, “letters being friends”, hard/soft or long/short sounds, “blend” as a noun, “curly c”, “kicking k”, a child-facing spelling rule, or letter names as a reading prompt in the spoken lines and played story pages. `that_says` is an unfortunate internal ID, but its current spoken text is exactly “That’s...”. Old examples of prohibited wording survive in comments and older diagnosis in `PEDAGOGY.md`; they are not current spoken dialogue. “What word do they make?” in `run_blend` refers to **sounds**, not letters, and does not breach the rule.

**A teacher-method conflict in an unplayed Help path.** `Dojo.tsx` Build and `Battle.tsx` both respond to the second Help press by saying `say_sounds` and then playing every `word.segs` sound while the child is spelling. The official word-building language says to stretch the *whole word* and let the child hear the sound at the active line, rather than segmenting the word for them. The bots did not exercise this Help branch, so the issue comes from the trigger code and the teacher-language source, not an observed spoken event. Fixes F27 and F29 replace the two branches.

**Words and terms for the youngest players.** `dojo_hello` defines *dojo*, but first appears at w2-4 rather than the first w1-4 dojo. *Streak*, *gem energy*, *petal*, *spelling* and *sound in the middle* are carried mainly by stock phrases; for a 3–4-year-old, the missing connection to an action or example is the problem. Baron’s w5-11 “How muddling!” is the only heard two-letter claim; the villain should not be the sole teacher of a core concept. “Spell the words to zap it!” in `battle_start` is clear after early building, but the first optional timed gem battle needs its own short bar demonstration. `sort_start` describes “the same sound” and “written in different ways” without first pointing to two specific spellings.

**Terms and audio construction.** The Flower uses *gem battle* and the battle scene calls an ordinary enemy a *battle*; that distinction is sound but needs the first ready-gem explanation. Rewards say *ninja energy* (`gem_energy`), while the intro says *ninja power* (`flower_i4`) for the same fill. Choose one term, preferably *ninja power* since the streak already uses it, and distinguish the gem’s fill from the ninja’s streak visually. `dojo_this_sound` says “And this is how we write it”; `how_we_spell` says “This is how we spell...”. Use *spell* when pointing at print, as in the official language. The log records fragment clips such as “This is a...” and “Tap the...”, followed by a separate word clip. They are intended to form a sentence, but the splices are audible to Jonas and vulnerable to interruption; w1-1 names pictures this way 17/45 times in the perfect/learner paths. Record whole recurring picture directions once the revised scripts are settled.

**Repetition that grates.** `gem_energy` plays 51/50 times and `book_new` 50/34, often at consecutive rewards without a new explanation. `listen_here` plays **207** times in the learner journey, including 15 in w1-4; it is a useful correction, but this volume comes from long repeated items. The same `sort_start` plus `same_sound_diff` pair opens nearly every sort (8/7 plus 9/9), without a fresh word pair. `say_sounds_read` plays 37/54 times across the early build levels. Preserve that Sounds~Write prompt, but shorten the *number of near-identical items* and avoid an extra whole-script preamble each time. Praise has variety on paper, yet “Ninja power!” plays 62/59 and “You did it!” 60/60; use them at meaningful tier/reward moments.
